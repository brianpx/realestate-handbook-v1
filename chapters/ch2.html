PART II: Find

2. Finding clients

Every lead source trades time for money or money for time. Know the trade you are making, track every source, and double down on what converts.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Build and work a sphere of influence list without feeling like a salesperson.
  • Compare lead sources by conversion rate, cost and exclusivity.
  • Run an open house as a lead-generation event rather than a waiting room.
  • Track cost per closed deal by source.

Sphere of influence and referrals

Your sphere of influence is everyone who already knows you and would take your call: family, friends, former colleagues, neighbors, your dentist, the parents at your kid's school, the people in your gym class. For most agents this is the highest-return source they will ever have, because trust is already built and the cost is only time.

Build the list first. Go through your phone contacts, social media connections, email history and holiday card list. Most people find 150 to 300 names. Import every one into Command and tag them by relationship so you can sort later.

Then work the list with value first. The order matters:

  1. Announce. Tell everyone you are in real estate and with whom. One message, personal, not a mass blast.
  2. Give. Send something useful: a neighborhood market update, a home maintenance checklist, an introduction to a good contractor.
  3. Ask. After two or three helpful touches, ask for introductions. Chapter 6 has the words.
  4. Repeat. Put the whole sphere on a touch plan so no one hears from you only when you want something.
● PITFALL
New agents often skip the sphere because calling friends feels awkward, then spend money on portal leads to avoid the discomfort. Your sphere converts at five to ten times the rate of a portal lead. The awkwardness fades after the tenth call. The ad bill does not.

Online leads

Portal leads (Zillow Premier Agent and similar), your own ads, and inquiries through your KW Agent Site or IDX website provide volume. They also convert at a far lower rate, because the person has no relationship with you and has often inquired with several agents at once. Two things decide whether online leads pay off:

  • Speed. The first agent to make real contact usually wins the conversation. Chapter 4 covers the five-minute response window.
  • Persistence. Most online leads are 6 to 18 months from a transaction. Without a long nurture plan you are paying to warm up a lead for someone else.

Do not rely on online leads exclusively, and do not buy them until your follow-up system is built and tested on your sphere. Chapter 3 says more about when paid lead generation makes sense.

Open houses

An open house is a lead-generation event with a listing as the excuse. Treat it that way and it will produce buyers who are active now and neighbors who may sell next year. Treat it as sitting in a house for three hours and it will produce nothing.

Before: Preview the home and 5 nearest comps. Invite 20 nearest neighbors door-to-door or in person. Set up digital sign-in on a tablet.
During: Greet every visitor, ask open search questions, capture contact details, and qualify timeline, area, financing, and agent status. Offer a concrete next step.
After: Follow up with every visitor within 24 hours (text evening, call next morning). Put every visitor on a SmartPlan and log the source.

Geographic farming

Farming means choosing one neighborhood of 300 to 500 homes and becoming the agent everyone there recognizes. It works through consistency: monthly mailers, door-knocking, market reports, and sponsoring block parties. Expect 6 to 12 months before listings appear. Pick a farm with a turnover rate of at least 6% a year.

Expired listings and for-sale-by-owners

Owners whose listing expired, or who are trying to sell alone, have already raised their hand. Lead with curiosity about what they want to do next, not with criticism of their last agent or price. This source rewards skilled, consistent prospectors.

Track every source

In Command, every contact carries a lead source. Fill it in every time. At the end of each month, count by source: contacts added, appointments held, closings, and dollars spent. The number you want is cost per closed deal.

● TRY IT TODAY
• Build your sphere list (Aim for 150 names). Import into Command with a "Sphere" tag.
• Send the announcement message to the first 20 people. Personal, two sentences, no sales pitch.
• Ask a colleague whether you can host their next open house. Schedule it.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Your sphere is the best-converting, cheapest source you will ever have. Work it first.
  • Online leads need speed and a long nurture. Without both, you pay to warm leads for others.
  • Open houses are events: invite neighbors, capture digitally, follow up within 24 hours.
  • Tag every contact with a source and measure cost per closed deal.
