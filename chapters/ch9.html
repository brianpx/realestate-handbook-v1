PART VI: Serve and Keep

A consultation is a conversation. An objection is a request for information. A closed client is the beginning of the relationship, not the end.

9. Working with clients and dealing with objections

A good consultation is a conversation, not a pitch. Ask, listen, then show how you work and why it helps them.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Run a buyer consultation and a seller consultation in a repeatable sequence.
  • Handle objections with curiosity using a four-step pattern.
  • Document every conversation so nothing is lost or disputed.

The consultation sequence

Both buyer and seller consultations follow the same shape. Set the frame, ask, listen, present, agree on next steps. Agents who skip the asking and go straight to presenting lose the client's trust before they have earned it.

Objections are questions in disguise

Most objections are requests for more information or reassurance. "Your commission is too high" usually means "show me what I get for it." "We want to wait" usually means "we are nervous about the market." Respond with curiosity, not defense. The four-step pattern below works for nearly every objection, and Table 9.2 applies it to the ones you will hear most.

  1. Acknowledge. "That makes sense." You are not agreeing; you are showing you heard.
  2. Clarify. "Can I ask what's behind that?" Find the real concern.
  3. Respond. Answer the real concern with facts, a story, or a demonstration.
  4. Check. "Does that address it?" Then stop talking.
● SCRIPT · "WE WANT TO INTERVIEW OTHER AGENTS."
"I think that's smart; this is a big decision. Can I ask what you'll be comparing? ... Great. Then let me make sure you leave today with those answers from me, so you have a fair comparison. And if you'd like, I'll follow up Thursday after you've met the others."
Purpose: stay in the running and set the follow-up. Next step: a scheduled call after the other interviews.

Objection quick reference

Treat each response as a starting framework, not a line to memorize. After you respond, ask a question that moves the conversation forward. Log the objection and your response in your CRM so the next follow-up is specific. Practice the three or four you hear most until they feel natural.

OBJECTIONRESPONSE FRAMEWORKGOAL OF THE RESPONSE
"I'm just looking."Acknowledge and offer value. "That's fine. Would it help if I sent a few options that match what you described, so you can see what's out there?"Keep the conversation open and useful.
"We're not ready yet."Explore timeline and stay in touch. "No problem. When do you think you might be ready? I can send occasional updates so you're informed when the time comes."Qualify timing and earn permission to nurture.
"I already have an agent."Respect and leave the door open. "Great, I'm glad you have help. If anything changes or you'd like a second opinion, feel free to reach out."Stay professional and memorable.
"Your commission is too high."Focus on value and net proceeds. "I understand. Let's look at what you actually net after marketing, negotiation and the sale. Most clients find the difference is worth it."Shift from fee to outcome.
"I want to wait for the market to improve."Provide data and options. "That's a common thought. Here's what similar homes have done recently. We can also look at what waiting might cost versus moving now."Educate without pressure.
"I need to think about it."Clarify the real concern. "Of course. What specifically would you like to think through? I'm happy to answer any questions that would help."Uncover the real objection.
"Can you just send me listings?"Agree and add a next step. "Absolutely. I'll send a few. What's the best way for me to follow up after you've had a chance to look?"Move from passive to engaged.
"I'm working with another agent on a home."Stay helpful and low-pressure. "No problem at all. If that one doesn't work out or you'd like another perspective later, I'm here."Preserve the relationship.
"You're new, aren't you?"Be honest and specific. "I am, and that means you get my full attention. My office and broker are behind every file I touch. What matters most to you in an agent?"Replace worry with evidence.
"We want to interview other agents."Support it and set the follow-up. "That's smart. What will you be comparing? Let me make sure you leave today with those answers from me, and I'll follow up after you've met the others."Stay in the running with a scheduled next step.

Document everything

After every consultation, offer discussion, price change or disclosure conversation, write a short note in your CRM: date, who, what was discussed, what was decided, what happens next. Two minutes of typing protects you from "but you said" months later and makes your follow-up specific. Chapter 11 covers what to document for legal protection.

After the transaction

Stay in touch. The clients who feel cared for after closing become your best source of future business. Chapter 10 shows the post-close plan. The habit starts at the closing table: thank them, ask for the introduction, and tell them to expect to hear from you.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. Frame, ask, listen, present, agree. Asking and listening come before presenting.
  2. Objections are requests for information. Acknowledge, clarify, respond, check.
  3. Document every conversation in your CRM the same day.
