PART VI: Serve and Keep

10. Keeping clients: long-term relationships

A database of past clients and sphere contacts who hear from you regularly is the most valuable asset you will build. It is also the cheapest one to maintain.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Put every closed client on a post-close SmartPlan the day of closing.
  • Add value in ways that do not feel like marketing.
  • Reactivate a database that has gone quiet.

The post-close plan

The average homeowner moves again within seven to ten years and knows several people who will move before then. The agent they remember gets those transactions. Being remembered is a schedule, not an accident.

Adding value without selling

The test for every touch: would the person be glad to receive it if they were never going to move again? Local insights, a reminder to change furnace filters, a list of trusted contractors, a note on a property tax deadline, an invitation to a neighborhood event. These pass the test. "Thinking of selling?" every month does not.

  • Vendor list. Keep a short list of a plumber, electrician, handyman, painter and cleaner you trust. Introducing them is a gift that costs you nothing.
  • Home value updates. Once a year, unprompted. Homeowners love knowing. Sellers come from this.
  • Life events. A new baby, a promotion, a graduation. A card beats a market update.

Reactivating a quiet database

If you have let your database go cold, do not apologize for it. Send one useful piece of market information to everyone, then start calling in small batches with a simple "thinking of you" and one question about how things are. Most people will be glad to hear from you. Put everyone you reach back on the quarterly plan so it does not go cold again.

● FIELD NOTE
Track one more number: referrals received per month. When this number grows, your business becomes less dependent on paid leads every year. Agents in their fifth year often get more than half their business this way.
