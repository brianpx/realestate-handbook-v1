3. Marketing and brand presence

Marketing should make people feel two things: you are the local expert, and you are the person who will take care of them. Consistency beats perfection.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Set up a home base with your Agent Site and Client App.
  • Keep a simple content calendar you can sustain for a year.
  • Market a listing so it produces buyer and seller leads, not just a sale.
  • Decide when paid advertising is worth it.

Your home base

Your Agent Site and Client App are the places you send everyone. The site holds your profile, your listings, and home search for your market. The app gives your clients a branded search experience that keeps you attached to every home they save. Set both up in your first week, with a professional photo, a short bio written in plain language, and your real contact details. Check that every link on your social profiles and email signature points to them.

A content calendar you can keep

People hire the agent they remember. Showing up regularly with useful information is how they remember you. Pick a schedule that you can keep on a busy week, then keep it. A workable minimum for a new agent:

● FIELD NOTE
You do not need to be a video personality. A 45-second phone video of you standing in front of a sold home, explaining one thing the buyer learned, outperforms a polished stock graphic. People hire people.

Market every listing thoroughly

A listing is the most powerful marketing asset you will hold. Good listing marketing sells the house and also generates buyers (who call about it) and sellers (neighbors who see you working). For every listing, at minimum:

  • Professional photography and a floor plan. Phone photos cost sellers money and cost you credibility.
  • A "coming soon" post and a "just listed" post, each with a call to action that leads to you.
  • Mailers or door-knocks to the 50 nearest homes before the open house.
  • A "just sold" update with a line about how many showings or offers it received. This is the single best seller-lead post you can make.

Paid advertising

Facebook and Instagram ads, Google ads and portal placements can all accelerate volume. They are worth it only when your follow-up system is already catching and nurturing leads reliably. The test is simple: if a new lead arrived at 9 p.m. tonight, would they get an immediate automated reply, a call before 9 a.m., and a 90-day plan without you having to remember? If not, buying leads is buying a leak.

When you do start, start small, run one source at a time for 90 days, and track cost per appointment and cost per closed deal before scaling.

● PITFALL
Spending on branding before spending time on conversations. Logos, car magnets and a custom website produce nothing on their own. Your first marketing budget is your calendar.

• What two feelings should your marketing create, and which of your current posts create them?

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. Agent Site and Client App are the home base. Send everyone there.
  2. A modest, consistent content schedule beats bursts of brilliance.
  3. Every listing should produce buyers and future sellers, not only a closing.
  4. Paid ads amplify a system. They cannot replace one.
