PART IV: Nurture

6. Scripts: conversations that convert

Scripts are starting points, not lines to recite. The goal is a natural conversation that moves the relationship one step forward.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Open a conversation with a new lead by phone and by text.
  • Ask your sphere for introductions without sounding like a salesperson.
  • Open buyer and seller consultations so the client knows what to expect.
  • Respond to "I'm just looking" and ask for referrals after closing.

How to use a script

Read it, rewrite it in your own words, then say it out loud until you do not need the paper. Record yourself on your phone and listen back; you will hear every place where you rush or apologize. Each script below has a purpose and a next step. If you reach the next step, the script worked, even if you said none of the exact words.

● SCRIPT · NEW LEAD BY PHONE (SPEED-TO-LEAD)
"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Your Brokerage]. I just saw your inquiry about [address or area]. Do you have a couple of minutes so I can answer your questions and see how I can help?"
Purpose: make human contact fast. Next step: a 10-minute conversation now, or a scheduled call today.
● SCRIPT · NEW LEAD BY TEXT
"Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Your Brokerage]. Thanks for your interest in [property or area]. Happy to help. What's the best time for a quick call today?"
Purpose: get a reply. Next step: a time for a call. If they answer with a question, answer it and ask one back.
● SCRIPT · SPHERE REFERRAL ASK
"I've been working with several families in the area, and I realized I haven't asked you yet. Do you know anyone who might be thinking about buying or selling in the next year? I'd love the chance to help them the way I help my clients."
Purpose: an introduction. Next step: a name and permission to reach out, or "I'll keep you in mind." Either is a win. Thank them and move on.
● SCRIPT · BUYER CONSULTATION OPENING
"My goal today is to understand exactly what you're looking for, walk you through how the process works, and make sure you feel confident about the steps ahead. Sound good?"
Purpose: set the frame. Next step: their agreement, then your questions. Cover timeline, motivation, financing and must-haves before you talk about homes.
● SCRIPT · SELLER CONSULTATION OPENING
"I'd like to learn about your goals and timeline first. Then I'll show you what similar homes have done and how I'd market yours. By the end you should have a clear picture of whether I'm the right fit."
Purpose: earn the right to present. Next step: their goals, then your comparables, then your marketing plan, then the listing agreement.
● SCRIPT · OBJECTION: "I'M JUST LOOKING."
"That's completely fine. Most people start that way. Would it help if I sent you a few homes that match what you described, so you can see what's out there? No pressure either way."
Purpose: keep the door open and earn permission to stay in touch. Next step: their email and a follow-up plan.
● SCRIPT · POST-CLOSE REFERRAL REQUEST
"It's been a pleasure working with you. If you know anyone else who might be thinking about a move, I'd be grateful for the introduction. I'll take great care of them."
Purpose: turn a happy client into a source. Next step: a name, or a review on your Agent Site. Ask at the closing table and again at the 30-day check-in.

Three rules for any script

  • Say their name and the specific thing they asked about. Specificity proves you read the inquiry.
  • Ask one question at the end. A message with no question gets no reply.
  • Stop talking. After the question, wait. Silence is where the other person decides to engage.
● TRY IT TODAY
• Rewrite the new-lead phone script in your own words. Record yourself saying it three times.
• Role-play the buyer consultation opening with a colleague. Swap roles.
• Use the sphere referral ask on three calls this week and log the outcomes in your CRM.
● CHECKPOINT
• What is the "next step" for each of the seven scripts?
• What three rules apply to every script?
• Can you deliver the seller consultation opening without looking at the page?

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. A script succeeds when it reaches its next step, not when you say it word for word.
  2. Be specific, ask one question, then be quiet.
  3. Practice out loud and on recording. Reading is not rehearsal.
