How to use this handbook

Most new agents do not fail for lack of effort. They fail because effort is scattered across activities that do not compound. This handbook gives you one system with three outcomes, and every chapter serves one of them:

  • Find. Generate a steady flow of conversations with people who may buy or sell.
  • Nurture. Follow up often enough, and helpfully enough, that you are the agent they call when they are ready.
  • Keep. Stay useful after closing so past clients send you the next ones.

PART I: Foundations

1. Mindset and business foundations

Real estate is a business, not a job. Your results come from the habits and systems you build, not from waiting for the phone to ring.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Tell leading indicators from lagging indicators, and set goals on the right one.
  • Work backward from an income goal to a daily number of conversations.
  • Treat KW Command as your daily headquarters rather than an occasional tool.

You own a business now

When you were an employee, somebody else found the customers, set the schedule and decided what mattered. All three jobs are now yours. Nobody will assign you a task list on Monday morning, and no one will notice if you spend a week on busywork. That freedom is the reason most people enter the business and the reason most leave it.

The agents who last think of themselves as owners of a small company with one product: trusted guidance through a large financial decision. A company needs a way to find customers, a way to stay in touch with them, and a way to keep them. That is the whole structure of this book.

Leading and lagging indicators

A lagging indicator tells you what already happened. Closings, commission income and the number of listings you hold are all lagging. By the time they move, the work that moved them is weeks or months old.

A leading indicator is an activity you control today that predicts the lagging number later. Conversations held, appointments set, follow-up tasks completed and contacts added to your database are leading. You can do all of them tomorrow morning regardless of the market.

New agents who set goals only on closings get discouraged, because closings do not respond for 60 to 120 days. Set your goals on activity, review them weekly, and let the closings arrive on their own schedule.

Command is your headquarters

KW Command is where your contacts, tasks, SmartPlans, Opportunities and marketing live. The habit that matters most in your first 90 days is simple: open Command first thing every morning and update it before you stop working. Contacts that live in your phone, sticky notes and inbox are contacts you will forget. Agents who treat Command as non-negotiable stay organized. Those who do not fall behind within a quarter and rarely recover the lost leads.

● CHECKPOINT
• Which three numbers will you track every week, and why are none of them closings?

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Closings lag by two to four months. Measure the activity that produces them.
  • Work backward from income to a daily conversation target you can hit on your worst day.
  • Command is the single source of truth. Open it first, update it last.
PART II: Find

2. Finding clients

Every lead source trades time for money or money for time. Know the trade you are making, track every source, and double down on what converts.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Build and work a sphere of influence list without feeling like a salesperson.
  • Compare lead sources by conversion rate, cost and exclusivity.
  • Run an open house as a lead-generation event rather than a waiting room.
  • Track cost per closed deal by source.

Sphere of influence and referrals

Your sphere of influence is everyone who already knows you and would take your call: family, friends, former colleagues, neighbors, your dentist, the parents at your kid's school, the people in your gym class. For most agents this is the highest-return source they will ever have, because trust is already built and the cost is only time.

Build the list first. Go through your phone contacts, social media connections, email history and holiday card list. Most people find 150 to 300 names. Import every one into Command and tag them by relationship so you can sort later.

Then work the list with value first. The order matters:

  1. Announce. Tell everyone you are in real estate and with whom. One message, personal, not a mass blast.
  2. Give. Send something useful: a neighborhood market update, a home maintenance checklist, an introduction to a good contractor.
  3. Ask. After two or three helpful touches, ask for introductions. Chapter 6 has the words.
  4. Repeat. Put the whole sphere on a touch plan so no one hears from you only when you want something.
● PITFALL
New agents often skip the sphere because calling friends feels awkward, then spend money on portal leads to avoid the discomfort. Your sphere converts at five to ten times the rate of a portal lead. The awkwardness fades after the tenth call. The ad bill does not.

Online leads

Portal leads (Zillow Premier Agent and similar), your own ads, and inquiries through your KW Agent Site or IDX website provide volume. They also convert at a far lower rate, because the person has no relationship with you and has often inquired with several agents at once. Two things decide whether online leads pay off:

  • Speed. The first agent to make real contact usually wins the conversation. Chapter 4 covers the five-minute response window.
  • Persistence. Most online leads are 6 to 18 months from a transaction. Without a long nurture plan you are paying to warm up a lead for someone else.

Do not rely on online leads exclusively, and do not buy them until your follow-up system is built and tested on your sphere. Chapter 3 says more about when paid lead generation makes sense.

Open houses

An open house is a lead-generation event with a listing as the excuse. Treat it that way and it will produce buyers who are active now and neighbors who may sell next year. Treat it as sitting in a house for three hours and it will produce nothing.

Before: Preview the home and 5 nearest comps. Invite 20 nearest neighbors door-to-door or in person. Set up digital sign-in on a tablet.
During: Greet every visitor, ask open search questions, capture contact details, and qualify timeline, area, financing, and agent status. Offer a concrete next step.
After: Follow up with every visitor within 24 hours (text evening, call next morning). Put every visitor on a SmartPlan and log the source.

Geographic farming

Farming means choosing one neighborhood of 300 to 500 homes and becoming the agent everyone there recognizes. It works through consistency: monthly mailers, door-knocking, market reports, and sponsoring block parties. Expect 6 to 12 months before listings appear. Pick a farm with a turnover rate of at least 6% a year.

Expired listings and for-sale-by-owners

Owners whose listing expired, or who are trying to sell alone, have already raised their hand. Lead with curiosity about what they want to do next, not with criticism of their last agent or price. This source rewards skilled, consistent prospectors.

Track every source

In Command, every contact carries a lead source. Fill it in every time. At the end of each month, count by source: contacts added, appointments held, closings, and dollars spent. The number you want is cost per closed deal.

● TRY IT TODAY
• Build your sphere list (Aim for 150 names). Import into Command with a "Sphere" tag.
• Send the announcement message to the first 20 people. Personal, two sentences, no sales pitch.
• Ask a colleague whether you can host their next open house. Schedule it.
● CHECKPOINT
• Why do sphere contacts convert so much better than portal leads?
• What three things must be in place before you pay for online leads?
• What is your 24-hour follow-up standard after an open house?

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Your sphere is the best-converting, cheapest source you will ever have. Work it first.
  • Online leads need speed and a long nurture. Without both, you pay to warm leads for others.
  • Open houses are events: invite neighbors, capture digitally, follow up within 24 hours.
  • Tag every contact with a source and measure cost per closed deal.
PART II: Find

3. Marketing and brand presence

Marketing should make people feel two things: you are the local expert, and you are the person who will take care of them. Consistency beats perfection.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Set up a home base with the KW Agent Site and the KW App.
  • Keep a simple content calendar you can sustain for a year.
  • Market a listing so it produces buyer and seller leads, not just a sale.
  • Decide when paid advertising is worth it.

Your home base

Your KW Agent Site and the KW App are the places you send everyone. The site holds your profile, listings, and search. The app gives clients a branded search experience attached to you. Set both up in your first week with photo, bio, and contact details.

● FIELD NOTE
You do not need to be a video personality. A 45-second phone video of you standing in front of a sold home, explaining one thing the buyer learned, outperforms a polished stock graphic. People hire people.

Market every listing thoroughly

A listing is the most powerful marketing asset you will hold. Good listing marketing sells the house and generates buyers and sellers. Minimum requirements: professional photography/floor plan, "coming soon" and "just listed" posts with calls to action, mailer/door-knocks to 50 nearest homes, and a "just sold" update.

Paid advertising

Ads are worth it only when your follow-up system is already catching and nurturing leads reliably. If a lead arrived at 9 p.m., would they get an automated reply, a call before 9 a.m., and a 90-day plan without you remembering? If not, buying leads is buying a leak.

● PITFALL
Spending on branding before spending time on conversations. Logos, car magnets and custom websites produce nothing on their own. Your first marketing budget is your calendar.
● TRY IT TODAY
• Complete your Agent Site profile and send yourself the KW App invitation.
• Record one 45-second video answering the question you were asked most often this week.
• Write next month's four weekly posts in one sitting and schedule them.
● CHECKPOINT
• What two feelings should your marketing create, and which of your current posts create them?
• Name the five posts every listing should generate.
• What must be true before you buy leads?

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Agent Site and KW App are the home base. Send everyone there.
  • A modest, consistent content schedule beats bursts of brilliance.
  • Every listing should produce buyers and future sellers, not only a closing.
  • Paid ads amplify a system. They cannot replace one.

Speed-to-lead

Figure 4. Speed-to-Lead Response Window

• Under 5 Minutes: Highest conversion window.
• 5 to 30 Minutes: Strong conversion.
• After 30 Minutes: Sharp drop-off. Most agents respond in hours; top producers respond in minutes.

The standard has two layers:

  1. Automated first touch (within 1 minute): SmartPlan sends immediate text and email.
  2. Live call (within 5 minutes): Turn on app notifications and call immediately. If unavailable, send a personal voice note or specific text.

PART IV: Nurture

5. Nurturing leads

Most leads convert between the fifth and twelfth touch. Most agents quit after the second. The gap between those two facts is where your business is.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Explain why multi-touch follow-up works and plan 8 to 12 touches over 90 days.
  • Build SmartPlans that mix calls, texts, emails and value pieces.
  • Qualify a lead early with four questions.
  • Run a weekly pipeline review so nobody falls through the cracks.

Why one call is never enough

Online leads are usually months from a decision. 80% of conversions occur between touches 5 and 12.

The weekly pipeline review

Every Friday, review your active contacts and ask:
1. When did I last have a real two-way conversation with this person?
2. What is the next step, and is it scheduled?
3. Is this person in the right stage?

Anyone with no conversation in 14 days and no scheduled step gets a call Monday.

● TRY IT TODAY
• Build the new-lead SmartPlan from Appendix B in Command and apply it to leads from the last 30 days.
• Call the 5 oldest leads in your database that never got a plan. Ask the 4 qualifying questions.
• Put a recurring 30-minute "Pipeline review" block on Friday afternoons.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Most conversions happen between touch 5 and touch 12. Plan for at least 90 days.
  • Front-load touches, mix channels, give value every third touch.
  • Qualify early on timeline, motivation, financing and representation.
  • Review pipeline every Friday. Fourteen quiet days triggers a call.
PART IV: Nurture

6. Scripts: conversations that convert

Scripts are starting points, not lines to recite. The goal is a natural conversation that moves the relationship one step forward.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Open a conversation with a new lead by phone and text.
  • Ask your sphere for introductions without sounding like a salesperson.
  • Open buyer and seller consultations.
  • Respond to "I'm just looking" and ask for post-close referrals.
● SCRIPT · NEW LEAD BY PHONE (SPEED-TO-LEAD)
"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with Keller Williams. I just saw your inquiry about [address or area]. Do you have a couple of minutes so I can answer your questions and see how I can help?"
Purpose: human contact fast. Next step: 10-minute conversation or scheduled call.
● SCRIPT · NEW LEAD BY TEXT
"Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with Keller Williams. Thanks for your interest in [property or area]. Happy to help. What's the best time for a quick call today?"
Purpose: get a reply. Next step: scheduled call time.
● SCRIPT · SPHERE REFERRAL ASK
"I've been working with several families in the area, and I realized I haven't asked you yet. Do you know anyone who might be thinking about buying or selling in the next year? I'd love the chance to help them the way I help my clients."
● SCRIPT · BUYER CONSULTATION OPENING
"My goal today is to understand exactly what you're looking for, walk you through how the process works, and make sure you feel confident about the steps ahead. Sound good?"
● SCRIPT · SELLER CONSULTATION OPENING
"I'd like to learn about your goals and timeline first. Then I'll show you what similar homes have done and how I'd market yours. By the end you should have a clear picture of whether I'm the right fit."
● SCRIPT · OBJECTION: "I'M JUST LOOKING."
"That's completely fine. Most people start that way. Would it help if I sent you a few homes that match what you described, so you can see what's out there? No pressure either way."
● SCRIPT · POST-CLOSE REFERRAL REQUEST
"It's been a pleasure working with you. If you know anyone else who might be thinking about a move, I'd be grateful for the introduction. I'll take great care of them."

Three rules for any script

  1. Say their name and the specific item they inquired about.
  2. Ask one question at the end.
  3. Stop talking (silence allows them to engage).

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • A script succeeds when it reaches its next step, not when said word-for-word.
  • Be specific, ask one question, then be quiet.
  • Practice out loud and record yourself.

PART VI: Serve and Keep

9. Working with clients and dealing with objections

The Consultation Sequence: 1. Frame ➔ 2. Ask ➔ 3. Listen ➔ 4. Present ➔ 5. Agree.

The 4-Step Objection Pattern

  1. Acknowledge. "That makes sense."
  2. Clarify. "Can I ask what's behind that?"
  3. Respond. Answer with facts or data.
  4. Check. "Does that address it?" (Then stop talking).
OBJECTIONRESPONSE FRAMEWORKGOAL
"I'm just looking."Acknowledge & offer value: "Would it help if I sent a few options matching your description?"Keep conversation open.
"We're not ready yet."Explore timeline: "When do you think you might be ready? I can send occasional updates."Qualify timing & earn nurture right.
"I already have an agent."Respect & stay open: "Great. If anything changes or you'd like a second opinion, reach out."Stay professional & memorable.
"Your commission is too high."Focus on net proceeds: "Let's look at what you actually net after marketing and negotiation."Shift fee to outcome.
"I want to wait for the market to improve."Provide data: "Here is what similar homes have done recently... let's compare costs."Educate without pressure.
"I need to think about it."Clarify: "Of course. What specifically would you like to think through?"Uncover real objection.
"Can you just send me listings?"Agree + next step: "I'll send a few. What's the best way to follow up after you look?"Move passive to engaged.
"You're new, aren't you?"Honest & specific: "I am, and that means you get my full attention backed by my Market Center."Replace worry with evidence.
"We want to interview other agents."Support & set follow-up: "That's smart. What will you compare? I'll follow up Thursday."Stay in running with next step.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Frame, ask, listen, present, agree. Asking/listening take half the meeting.
  • Objections are requests for information. Acknowledge, clarify, respond, check.
  • Document every conversation in Command the same day.

11. Legal protection and professional standards

Agency Disclosure: Disclose at first substantive contact and keep signed copies.
Fair Housing: Describe property features, never demographics ("4 bedrooms, fenced yard" vs "great for families"). Treat every inquiry identically.
What to Document: Agency disclosures, offers/counters, pricing discussions, delivered disclosures, inspection choices, and professional referrals.

Appendix D: Glossary

8x8: Eight touches over eight weeks to establish a new relationship.
Agency disclosure: Required form explaining representation duties.
Command: KW platform for contacts, tasks, SmartPlans, Opportunities, marketing, and transactions.
Conversation: Two-way exchange about real estate with someone who could buy, sell or refer.
Lagging indicator: Result reporting past work (closings, income).
Leading indicator: Controllable daily activity predicting future results (calls, appointments).
Opportunities: Command pipeline tracking transactions across stages.
SmartPlan: Automated sequence of touches in Command (texts, emails, call reminders).
Speed-to-lead: Elapsed time between lead inquiry and first response.
Sphere of influence: Everyone who knows you and would take your call.
