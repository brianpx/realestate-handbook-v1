PART II: Find

3. Marketing and brand presence

In Chapter 2 you chose where your first conversations will come from. Chapter 3 is about what people see once you have started them. A friend you called last week will look you up. A neighbor who picks up your mailer will search your name. Before they decide whether to call you, they will decide who you are.

Marketing should make them feel two things: you are the local expert, and you are the person who will take care of them. Consistency beats perfection. This chapter shows you how to be easy to find, easy to remember and easy to trust, without spending money you do not yet need to spend. Your first marketing budget is your calendar.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Write one sentence that says who you help and where, and use it in your profile, bio and first post.
  • Plan a four-week content calendar you can keep on your busiest week.
  • List the five posts every listing should produce, and explain how each leads back to you.
  • Apply the 9 p.m. test to decide whether you are ready to pay for leads.

Your home base

Your Agent Site (your public website profile, listings and home search) and your Client App (a branded search app for your clients that keeps you attached to every home they save) are the places you send everyone. If your brokerage calls them something else, or does not provide them, use your public profile page and a link to your home search. Set them up in your first week, with a professional photo, a short bio written in plain language, and your real contact details. Check that every link on your social profiles and email signature points to your Agent Site.

What people should say about you

For a new agent, a brand is a promise stated in one sentence, plus proof. Fill in the blanks: "I help [who] [do what] in [where]." For example: "I help first-time buyers in Eastside feel confident about their first offer." Use that sentence in your bio, your profile and your first post. Every post after that is proof of the sentence.

Proof is the problem when you have no sold homes. Until you have your own, borrow a colleague's with their permission, or profile a local business, a school or a park. Credibility starts with knowing the place.

A content calendar you can keep

People hire the agent they remember. Showing up regularly with useful information is how they remember you. Pick a schedule that you can keep on a busy week, then keep it. A workable minimum for a new agent:

● FIELD NOTE
You do not need to be a video personality. A 45-second phone video of you standing in front of a home you toured or a place you know well, explaining one useful thing, tends to outperform a polished stock graphic. Once you have a sold home of your own, stand in front of that. People hire people.

Market every listing thoroughly

A listing is the most powerful marketing asset you will hold. Good listing marketing sells the house and also generates buyers (who call about it) and sellers (neighbors who see you working). For every listing, at minimum:

  • Professional photography and a floor plan. Phone photos cost sellers money and cost you credibility.
  • A "coming soon" post and a "just listed" post, each with a call to action that leads to you.
  • Mailers or door-knocks to the 50 nearest homes before the open house.
  • A "just sold" update with a line about how many showings or offers it received. This is the single best seller-lead post you can make.

Your broker has rules for what must appear on every ad, such as the brokerage name and your license number, and for how listing photos and Fair Housing language may be used. Ask before you post. Chapter 11 covers the standards.

Paid advertising

Facebook and Instagram ads, Google ads and portal placements can all accelerate volume. They are worth it only when your follow-up system is already catching and nurturing leads reliably. The test is simple: if a new lead arrived at 9 p.m. tonight, would they get an immediate automated reply, a call before 9 a.m., and a 90-day plan without you having to remember? You will build all three in Chapters 4 and 5. Until you have, hold off: buying leads before then is buying a leak.

When you do start, start small, run one source at a time for 90 days, and track cost per appointment and cost per closed deal before scaling.

● PITFALL
Spending on branding before spending time on conversations. Logos, car magnets and a custom website produce nothing on their own. Your first marketing budget is your calendar.
● TRY IT TODAY
• Write your one-sentence promise. Complete your public profile and send yourself the client app invitation, if your brokerage provides one, to see what a client sees.
• Pick the question for your video from your sphere conversations in Chapter 2. Record one 45-second video answering it and post it to your sphere list.
• Write next month's eight posts (two a week for four weeks) in one sitting, make every post answer your promise, and schedule them.
• Rehearse a listing: choose any currently listed home near you. List the five posts you would make, from "coming soon" to "just sold", and write the first line of each.
● CHECKPOINT
• What two feelings should your marketing create, and which of your current posts create them?
• Name the five posts every listing should generate.
• Which of your Chapter 2 sources would you add paid ads to first, and what would you track for 90 days?

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. A one-sentence promise plus proof is your brand. Your Agent Site and Client App are the home base. Send everyone there.
  2. A modest, consistent content schedule beats bursts of brilliance.
  3. Every listing should produce buyers and future sellers, not only a closing.
  4. Paid ads amplify a system. They cannot replace one.
