PART II: Find

Where clients come from, what each source costs, and how to make your name mean "local expert" before anyone needs you.

2. Finding clients

In Chapter 1 you worked out that your income goal needs roughly 16 conversations a week. That is a number. It is not yet a plan, because the next question is the practical one: with whom?

Every lead source trades time for money or money for time. Some cost nothing but nerve; some cost a monthly bill. This chapter shows you the five sources new agents use, what each demands of you, and how to start with the one that is cheapest and works best: the people who already know your name. You are not finding clients yet. You are finding conversations that become clients.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • List at least 150 people in your sphere and contact them in the order announce, give, ask, repeat.
  • Choose the first two lead sources to work, based on what each costs in time and money and what you can follow up on today.
  • Run an open house in three phases (before, during, after) so that every visitor enters a follow-up plan.
  • Calculate cost per closed deal for one source from four monthly counts.

Sphere of influence and referrals

Your sphere of influence is everyone who already knows you and would take your call: family, friends, former colleagues, neighbors, your dentist, the parents at your kid's school, the people in your gym class. For most agents this is the highest-return source they will ever have, because trust is already built and the cost is only time.

Build the list first. Go through your phone contacts, social media connections, email history and holiday card list. Most people find 150 to 300 names. Import every one into your CRM and tag them by relationship so you can sort later. If your CRM is not set up yet (Chapter 4 covers it), build the list in a spreadsheet and import it later.

Then work the list with value first. The order matters:

  1. Announce. Tell everyone you are in real estate and with whom. One message, personal, not a mass blast. Send it as a personal call or text, not through an automated tool, and check your broker's outreach rules first (Chapter 11).
  2. Give. Send something useful: a neighborhood market update, a home maintenance checklist, an introduction to a good contractor.
  3. Ask. After two or three helpful touches, ask for introductions. Chapter 6 has the words, including the announcement.
  4. Repeat. Put the whole sphere on a touch plan so no one hears from you only when you want something.
● PITFALL
New agents often skip the sphere because calling friends feels awkward, then spend money on portal leads to avoid the discomfort. Your sphere often converts far better than a portal lead. The awkwardness fades after the tenth call. The ad bill does not.

Online leads

Portal leads (Zillow Premier Agent and similar), your own ads, and inquiries through your Agent Site or IDX website provide volume. They also convert at a far lower rate, because the person has no relationship with you and has often inquired with several agents at once. Two things decide whether online leads pay off:

  • Speed. The first agent to make real contact usually wins the conversation. Chapter 4 covers the five-minute response window (for now, reply as fast as you can).
  • Persistence. Most online leads are 6 to 18 months from a transaction. Without a long nurture plan you are paying to warm up a lead for someone else.

Do not rely on online leads exclusively, and do not buy them until you pass the 9 p.m. test in Chapter 3: an immediate automated reply, a call by the next morning, and a 90-day plan, all running without you having to remember. Build and test that system on your sphere first.

Open houses

An open house is a lead-generation event with a listing as the excuse. Treat it that way and it will produce buyers who are active now and neighbors who may sell next year. Treat it as sitting in a house for three hours and it will produce nothing.

The sequence matters. Before is about being able to answer a price question on the spot. During is about capturing people, not selling to them. After is where the leads are made.

Before

  • Preview the home and the five nearest comparable sales so you can answer price questions on the spot.
  • Invite the 20 nearest neighbors in person or with a door-knock the day before. "Would you like to see what your neighbor's home is listed for?" is an easy opener.
  • Set up digital sign-in on a tablet that feeds your CRM. Paper sign-in sheets get lost.

During

  • Greet every visitor, ask one open question about their search, and capture name, phone and email.
  • Ask the qualifying questions in conversation (Chapter 5 teaches the four; for now, ask when they hope to move and whether they are working with an agent).
  • Offer a concrete next step: a list of similar homes, a buyer consultation, or a neighbor's home value report.

After

  • Follow up with every visitor within 24 hours. A text the same evening and a call the next morning is the standard.
  • Put every visitor on a follow-up plan. The visitor who was "just looking" today is often the buyer in four months.
  • Log the open house and its visitors as a lead source so you can measure it.

Geographic farming

Farming means choosing one neighborhood of 300 to 500 homes and becoming the agent everyone there recognizes. It works through consistency: monthly mailers, door-knocking, a neighborhood market report, sponsoring the block party. Expect six to twelve months before listings appear, so farm only if you can afford the wait. As a rule of thumb many agents use, pick a farm with a turnover rate of at least 6 percent a year, few dominant agents, and homes at a price point that pays you fairly for the effort.

Expired listings and for-sale-by-owners

Owners whose listing expired, or who are trying to sell alone, have already raised their hand. Many agents will call them, so the approach has to be respectful and persistent. Lead with curiosity about what they want to do next, not with criticism of their last agent or their price. This source rewards skilled, consistent prospectors and frustrates everyone else. A simple opener is: "I saw your home came off the market. What would you like to happen next?" Treat it as an advanced source and postpone it until your sphere and follow-up are working.

● BEFORE YOU CALL
Cold calls and texts to these owners can fall under Do Not Call and consent rules, and the rules differ by state. Ask your broker which lists you may use, what to scrub against, and when you may call or text. This is a pointer, not legal advice. Chapter 11 covers the habits.

Referral partners

Lenders, attorneys, other agents and relocation contacts meet people before they pick an agent. For a new agent with no past clients, they are the next-best source after your sphere. Introduce yourself, ask how they like to work, and refer to them when it helps your client. Ask your broker about any rules on referral fees before money or favors change hands.

Track every source

In your CRM, every contact carries a lead source. Fill it in every time. At the end of each month, count by source: contacts added, appointments held, closings, and dollars spent. The number you want is cost per closed deal: (dollars spent + your hours at a rate you choose) ÷ closings.

Worked example, open houses, month one. Four houses, 38 visitors captured, 6 appointments, and one closing in month five. Cost: $120 in materials plus 24 hours at $25 an hour, or $720. Cost per closed deal: $720 ÷ 1 = $720. After six months you will know which sources deserve more of your week and which to stop. Chapter 3 picks up with how people will see you once those conversations start.

● TRY IT TODAY
• Take your weekly conversation number from Chapter 1 and split it across your sources. A new agent might start with: sphere 10, open house follow-ups 3, other 3. Write the split on your card from Chapter 1.
• Build your sphere list. Aim for 150 names. Import them into your CRM with a "Sphere" tag, or keep them in a spreadsheet until your CRM is ready.
• Send the announcement message (Chapter 6 has the script) to the first 20 people. Personal, two sentences, no sales pitch.
• Ask a colleague whether you can host their next open house. Ask what you are allowed to say about the listing and how leads are shared. Schedule it.
● CHECKPOINT
• Why do sphere contacts convert so much better than portal leads?
• What has to be true before you pay for online leads? (Use the 9 p.m. test from Chapter 3.)
• What is your 24-hour follow-up standard after an open house?

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. Your sphere is the best-converting, cheapest source you will ever have. Work it first.
  2. Online leads need speed and a long nurture. Without both, you are paying to warm leads for other agents.
  3. Open houses are events: invite neighbors, capture digitally, follow up within 24 hours.
  4. Tag every contact with a source and measure cost per closed deal.
