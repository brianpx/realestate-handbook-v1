PART VII: Execute

13. Resources and next steps

You began with a goal and a question: what do I do on a Tuesday morning? You now have a number to hit, a place to record it, a plan for every lead, words to say, a calendar to protect, and a way to stay in touch after the closing. That is a business, and it is yours.

This handbook is a starting point. The next ninety days are where it becomes real, and the ninety after that are where you replace the book's examples with your own numbers. Consistency with a simple system outperforms occasional brilliance. The pages that follow give you the week ahead, the people to lean on, and the two habits that matter more than everything else.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Review your first 90 days against the scorecard and identify what worked.
  • Set the next 90 days' targets from your own conversion ratios, not the book's examples.
  • Choose the mentor, training and professional contacts you will use in your first year.

Do these this week

  • Pick one chapter, do its exercise, and move to the next.
  • Log in to your CRM and complete the guided setup.
  • Download the CRM mobile app and turn on new-lead notifications.
  • Ask your broker or a mentor for follow-up plan recommendations and the local training calendar.
  • Put the Friday scorecard review in your calendar for the next 12 weeks.

Where to get help

  • Your office. Team leader, any coach your brokerage offers, and the agents who are two years ahead of you. Ask for a mentor.
  • Brokerage training. The ongoing classes at your office cover scripts, your CRM and lead generation in depth.
  • Your broker. For anything legal, contractual or unusual. Before, not after.
  • Your lender partners. A good lender teaches you financing and sends you referrals. Meet three and pick one. Ask your broker what is and is not allowed before you agree to anything.
  • This handbook's appendices. Appendix E has the interactive tools, and Appendix F has the essential links.

The two habits that matter most

If you remember nothing else from this handbook, remember these two. Speed-to-lead: respond in minutes, not hours. Multi-touch follow-up: stay in touch for 90 days and then forever. These two habits separate agents who grow from agents who stay stuck. Everything else in the book exists to make them easy. The seven lines on the card below are the system; these two are the first things to check when results stall.

Plan the next 90 days

  • Write a one-paragraph review of your first 90 days: what moved, what did not, what surprised you. Put it in the CRM as a note dated day 90. If you are still reading, write it for the first 30.
  • Replace three of the book's example numbers with your own: conversations per appointment (Chapter 1), conversion by source (Chapter 2), touches before a reply (Chapter 5). Write the new ratio next to the old one.
  • Choose one person to share the review with: your broker, a mentor or an accountability partner (Chapter 8). Put a date on it.
  • Write one implementation intention: "On [day], at [time], I will [action]." Make it next Monday morning.

THE WHOLE HANDBOOK ON ONE CARD

  1. Measure activity, not closings. Three or four real conversations a day.
  2. Work your sphere first. Buy leads only when the system can catch them.
  3. Your CRM is the only list. Open it first, update it last.
  4. Respond to new leads in minutes. Then follow up 13 times over 90 days.
  5. Ask, listen, present, agree. Objections are questions.
  6. Every closed client goes on a post-close plan the day of closing.
  7. Review every Friday. Fix one thing a month. Ask your broker before anything unusual.

Nobody will assign you a task list on Monday morning. Now you have written your own.
