PART IV: Nurture

Most leads convert between the fifth and twelfth touch. Most agents quit after the second. The gap between those two facts is where your business is.

5. Nurturing leads

The first minute is the easy part. Your CRM sends a text, you pick up the phone, and, if you are quick, you reach someone. Then what? Most leads are not ready to decide on the first call. They are curious, early, unsure of financing, or waiting on a life event.

Follow-up that is frequent, varied and genuinely useful is what turns an inquiry into a client. This chapter shows you how to cross the gap: a 90-day plan that rotates calls, texts and emails; four questions that tell you how to help; and one Friday habit that keeps anyone from slipping away. Four ideas: why you follow up, what a good plan looks like, how to qualify, and how to check you are doing it.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Explain in two sentences why most leads convert after several touches, not one.
  • Plan a 13-touch, 90-day sequence that mixes channels and gives value every third touch.
  • Qualify a lead early with four questions, and stage them as active or nurture.
  • Run a weekly pipeline review so nobody falls through the cracks.

Why one call is never enough

A person who inquires about a home online is usually months from a decision. They are curious, early, unsure of financing, or waiting on a life event. One call cannot carry them through all of that. Many agents and industry trainers report that most conversions happen between the fifth and twelfth contact, spread across weeks or months; treat that as a working assumption and track your own. Most new agents stop after one or two attempts. The leads they abandon are the ones that would have closed for the agent who kept going.

● PITFALL

Treating "no answer" as "not interested". Unanswered calls are normal. A lead who has not replied to three touches is still a lead. The plan continues until they say stop or until they buy.

Designing a nurture plan

A good plan has four properties. Build every follow-up plan against them.

  • Front-loaded. Seven touches in the first 14 days, while interest is highest.
  • Mixed channel. Calls, texts and emails in rotation. Some people only answer texts. Some only read email. No single channel reaches everyone.
  • Value every third touch. A neighborhood report, a buyer guide, a "homes like the one you asked about" list, a short video. Give before you ask.
  • Long. At least 90 days of scheduled touches, then roll into the quarterly database plan. Nobody leaves your database unless they ask to.

Launch the right plan on every new lead the day it arrives, and launch the quarterly plan on your existing database this week. Table 5.1 shows which plan fits which contact, and Appendix B has day-by-day versions you can build into your CRM in an afternoon. The texting rules from Chapter 4 apply to every automated plan: permission first, quiet hours honored, and stop on the first opt-out. Chapter 11 has the detail.

● FIELD NOTE

Write your automated texts the way you would type them to a friend. "Hi Jordan, it's Sam with [Brokerage]. Still thinking about the Maple Street area?" outperforms anything that sounds like a newsletter.

Qualify early

Qualifying is not interrogating. It is asking the four questions that tell you how to help and how to prioritize your time. Work them naturally into your first real conversation.

Table 5.2. The four qualifying questions. Record the answers in your CRM on the contact the same day.
QUESTIONWHAT YOU ARE LEARNINGNATURAL PHRASING
TimelineWhen they want to move, and what is driving the date."When are you hoping to be settled in?"
MotivationWhy now, and how committed they are."What's prompting the move?"
FinancingWhether they are pre-approved, cash, or need a lender introduction."Have you had a chance to talk with a lender yet?"
RepresentationWhether another agent is already helping them."Are you working with anyone on this yet?"

Example. Jordan inquired about 14 Maple Street on Tuesday. On the day-7 call you learn: wants to move by next summer (timeline), a new job in the area (motivation), has not spoken to a lender (financing), not working with an agent (representation). Stage: nurture, with a lender introduction as your next step.

Use the answers to place every lead in an Opportunity stage. A pre-approved buyer moving in 60 days is an active opportunity and gets calls. A "maybe next year" lead is a nurture and gets the follow-up plan. Both are valuable. They just need different attention this week.

The weekly pipeline review

Every Friday, open your Opportunities pipeline and your follow-up plan task list and ask three questions about every active contact:

  1. When did I last have a real two-way conversation with this person?
  2. What is the next step, and is it scheduled?
  3. Is this person in the right stage?

Anyone with no conversation in 14 days and no scheduled next step gets a call on Monday. This 30-minute habit is the difference between a pipeline and a list of names.

● TRY IT TODAY

  • Build the new-lead follow-up plan from Appendix B in your CRM and apply it to the next lead you receive. If you have none yet, use a friend as a test lead. Do not apply a text plan to old leads unless you have their permission.
  • Call five people from your sphere list and ask the four qualifying questions in conversation (Table 5.2). Rehearse the day-7 call out loud first, using the Chapter 6 rules.
  • Put a recurring 30-minute "Pipeline review" block on Friday, right after your prospecting block (the Chapter 8 schedule). After your first review, tag each active contact with its source from Chapter 2.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. Most conversions happen between touch 5 and touch 12. Plan for at least 90 days.
  2. Front-load touches, mix channels, give value every third touch.
  3. Qualify early on timeline, motivation, financing and representation, and record it.
  4. Review the pipeline every Friday. Fourteen quiet days triggers a call.
