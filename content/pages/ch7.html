7. Common errors and pitfalls of new agents

You have now met the core of the system: find people, follow up, say the right words. Before the book turns to routines and conversations with clients, take a day to look at where this fails in real life.

New agents fall into the same six traps. They reinforce one another, which is why a bad month can become a bad year. The good news is that the correction is the same for all six, and you already have most of it. It works not because you become a different person, but because the system does the remembering. This chapter helps you see which trap is yours.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Score your own week against the six errors and name the one that costs you most.
  • Trace how one error leads to the next until the cycle closes.
  • Choose the single fix, from the chapter named in the Fix column, that you will make this week.

How the errors feed each other

These are not six separate problems. Slow follow-up loses leads. Lost leads mean an empty pipeline. An empty pipeline is discouraging. Discouragement makes the day unstructured, and an unstructured day produces slow follow-up. The loop runs on its own once it starts.

The one correction

You do not need six fixes. You need one system that makes the six errors hard to commit:

  1. Install your CRM and follow-up plans. Follow-up becomes automatic and documentation becomes the default.
  2. Protect a daily prospecting and follow-up block. Structure replaces willpower.
  3. Track activity, not closings. You can see progress every week, so the 90-day wait is bearable.
  4. Review every Friday. Small drifts get corrected before they become habits.

Most of these pitfalls shrink quickly once the system is in place. Expect to catch yourself in them, not to never fall into them.

Try it on Sam

Sam closed no deals in the first 30 days. This week: Monday a lead came in at 4 p.m. and Sam called at 6 p.m., two hours late. Tuesday Sam spent the morning on a logo. Wednesday's follow-up tasks went undone because "nothing is due." Thursday Sam had no appointment and felt low. Friday Sam did no pipeline review. Which of the six errors can you find? In what order did they occur? Which one single change on Monday would have broken the chain?

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. The six errors are predictable and linked. One starts the loop; the loop runs on its own.
  2. You do not need willpower. You need a system that makes the errors hard to commit.
  3. Your CRM, a protected block, activity tracking and a Friday review break the cycle.
