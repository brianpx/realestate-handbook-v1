12. Your first 90 days: daily, weekly and monthly plans

You have now met every part of the system. You know where your first conversations come from, where they are recorded, how you follow up, what you say, how you spend your mornings, how you run a consultation, and how you stay in touch after a closing. What remains is to put dates on it.

Ninety days is long enough to build the system and start a pipeline, and short enough to hold yourself to. This chapter turns the book into a calendar: three phases of thirty days, one scorecard every Friday, and one hour each month to decide where your time goes next. Closings usually follow in the second 90 days. When you finish this chapter, you will have a plan with your name on it.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Date a 90-day roadmap with checkpoints at days 30, 60 and 90.
  • Fill in the weekly scorecard each Friday and name the one number to move next week.
  • Run a one-hour monthly review that changes where your time goes.
  • Assemble your 90-Day Business Plan from the work you did in Chapters 1 to 11.

Your day, in one line: open the CRM, clear follow-ups, prospect to your number, hold appointments, close the CRM.

Days 1 to 30: install the system

Finish CRM setup (Chapter 4), import and tag contacts, launch the basic follow-up plans, and have at least 50 real conversations with your sphere (about 12 a week, the lower end of the Chapter 8 range). Hold one open house or one networking event. Run the speed-to-lead test. Do not worry about closings; worry about whether the system is on.

Days 31 to 60: build the rhythm

Hold the morning block at least four days out of five. Hit the Table 8.2 targets, including 15 to 25 conversations a week and 1 to 2 appointments set. Review your numbers every Friday. By day 60 you should have held your first consultations and signed a written buyer representation agreement before touring any homes. Chapter 11 covers buyer agreements. The content calendar from Chapter 3 should be running on its own.

Days 61 to 90: grow the pipeline

Your pipeline review should show at least five active opportunities. Write your first contract. Build the post-close plan before you need it. Count the results by lead source and decide, with data and the 9 p.m. test from Chapter 3, whether paid leads make sense next quarter. Record your conversion ratios so the next 90 days can be planned from real numbers.

Measure every week

Every Friday, fill in Appendix A. Table 8.2 is the same list of targets. Do not keep a second, shorter scorecard. If the full list is too much at first, start with the five core numbers from Chapter 8: conversations, appointments set, follow-ups completed, new contacts added and morning blocks held. Then name the one number you will move next week.

  • Outbound calls, conversations, follow-ups completed, appointments set and appointments held.
  • Open houses or events, new contacts added, and content posted.
  • Database updates and morning blocks, counted as days out of five.
  • Active opportunities, pipeline value, and referrals received.

Review every month

On the first working day of the month, spend one hour on four questions: Which source produced the most appointments per hour spent? Which follow-up plan got the most replies? Where did leads stall in the pipeline? What one change will I make this month? Write the answers in your CRM as a note titled "Monthly note" so next month's review can find and check them.

Assemble your 90-Day Business Plan

Every Try It in this book has produced one piece of your plan. Copy them onto one page:

  1. Your income goal and daily conversation number (Chapter 1).
  2. Your weekly conversation split by source (Chapter 2).
  3. Your content calendar (Chapter 3).
  4. Your CRM setup status and daily rhythm (Chapter 4).
  5. The three follow-up plans running (Chapter 5).
  6. Three scripts in your own words (Chapter 6).
  7. Your highest-scoring error and its fix (Chapter 7).
  8. Your weekly time blocks and scorecard numbers (Chapter 8).
  9. Your one-page consultation (Chapter 9).
  10. Your post-close plan (Chapter 10).
  11. Your compliance card: disclosure timing, consent rule, three "ask first" situations (Chapter 11).

Put each milestone on a date, and share the page with your broker or mentor.
