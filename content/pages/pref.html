Finding, Nurturing, and Keeping Clients

A practical guide for new real estate agents.

Who this is for

This handbook is for the agent in their first year: licensed, affiliated with a brokerage, and unsure what to do on a Tuesday morning when no one is calling. It does not teach you to write a contract or run a closing. Your broker and your state course do that. It teaches the other half of the job: how to find people who might buy or sell, stay in touch until they are ready, and earn the next introduction when they close. It is written for agents in any state, and the rules differ from state to state, so check your own state's rules with your broker before you rely on any rule in this book.

By day 90 you will have

  • A written income goal, turned into the number of conversations you hold each day.
  • A CRM that tells you every morning who to call.
  • Three follow-up plans running, so no lead goes quiet unnoticed.
  • Your first consultations behind you, and a pipeline you review every Friday.
  • A post-close plan ready to assign the day your first client goes under contract.

How to use this handbook

Most new agents do not fail for lack of effort. They fail because effort is scattered across activities that do not compound. This handbook gives you one system with three outcomes, and every chapter serves one of them:

  • Find. Generate a steady flow of conversations with people who may buy or sell.
  • Nurture. Follow up often enough, and helpfully enough, that you are the agent they call when they are ready.
  • Keep. Stay useful after closing so past clients send you the next ones.

The book is written to be worked, not just read. Each chapter opens with what you will be able to do by the end, teaches the idea, shows the words to say, and closes with an exercise and a checkpoint. Read a chapter, do the exercise the same day, and move on. Do not try to install everything in one weekend.

Reading plan

  • Week 1: Chapters 1, 2 and 3. Set your daily number, build your sphere list, and set up the home base people will find you through.
  • Week 2: Chapters 4, 5 and 6. Set up your CRM, put every lead on a plan, and learn the words to say.
  • Week 3: Chapters 7, 8 and 9. Audit your habits, protect your calendar, and practice consultations.
  • Week 4: Chapters 10, 11, 12 and 13. Add post-close plans and professional safeguards, then put dates on your first 90 days.

The plan follows the book, so each chapter builds on the ones before it. Two things cannot wait for week 4. The safeguards you need before you contact anyone are in the quick start below. And read Chapter 10 before your first client goes under contract, whatever week you are in. The post-close plan is built once, assigned on the day the contract goes under contract, and activated on closing day; it runs seven dated touches in the first year, starting with the closing-day note.

Quick start

If you want to start before you have read anything else, do these three things this week. None of them needs another chapter.

  1. Turn your income goal into a daily number of conversations with the calculator in Chapter 1, and write the number on a card where you work.
  2. Block 9:30 to 11:00 every weekday morning in your calendar and label it "Lead generation". Do not move it.
  3. Open your CRM first thing every morning and update it before you stop working.

Before your first outreach

Check these four things before you call, text or post for business. Chapter 11 covers each one in full, and your broker has your state's version.

  • Consent before automated texts. Send automated texts or calls only to people whose consent you have recorded in the CRM, and stop at the first opt-out.
  • Describe the property, not the people. In posts, listings and conversation, talk about the home and its location, never about who should live there or who lives there now. "Four bedrooms, fenced yard, two blocks from the park" is fine. "Great for families" is not.
  • Agency disclosure at first substantive contact. When a conversation moves from casual to real help with buying or selling, give your state's agency disclosure in plain language, and keep the signed copy.
  • Do-not-call. Do not call or text numbers on a do-not-call list, or outside the hours your state allows. Ask your broker which lists to check against.
