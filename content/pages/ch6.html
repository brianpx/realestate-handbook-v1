PART IV: Nurture

6. Scripts: conversations that convert

Your follow-up plan now says "Day 7: call." That is a task, not a conversation. Many new agents see the task, feel the pause before the call, and push it to tomorrow.

A script gets you past the pause. It is not something to read; it is a way to know the first sentence, the one question, and what "done" looks like. This chapter gives you the scripts that your follow-up plans, your sphere and your first consultations will call for, and a method to make them sound like you. The goal is a natural conversation that moves the relationship one step forward.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Open a conversation with a new lead by phone, text and voicemail, naming what they asked about.
  • Announce your new career to your sphere, and ask for introductions in a way that gives before it asks.
  • Apply three rules (be specific, ask one question, stop talking) to any message you write.
  • Rehearse a script out loud until you can say it in your own words.

How to use a script

Read it, rewrite it in your own words, then say it out loud until you do not need the paper. Record yourself on your phone and listen back; you will hear every place where you rush or apologize. Each script below has a purpose and a next step. If you reach the next step, the script worked, even if you said none of the exact words. Practice loop: read, rewrite, say aloud, record, listen, repeat.

Three rules to read every script by

  • Say their name and the specific thing they asked about. Specificity proves you read the inquiry.
  • Ask one question at the end. A message with no question rarely gets a reply.
  • Stop talking. After the question, wait. Silence is where the other person decides to engage.

Every script below follows them: name, specific detail, one question, then silence.

● SCRIPT · NEW LEAD BY PHONE (SPEED-TO-LEAD)
"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Your Brokerage]. I just saw your inquiry about [address or area]. Do you have a couple of minutes so I can answer your questions and see how I can help?"
Purpose: make human contact fast. Next step: a 10-minute conversation now, or a scheduled call today. If you are calling later than the same day, say "following up on your inquiry" instead of "I just saw".
● SCRIPT · VOICEMAIL (NO ANSWER)
"Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Your Brokerage]. I'm following up on your inquiry about [address or area]. I have a couple of ideas that might help. I'll try again tomorrow, or call me back at [number]."
Purpose: be specific and easy to answer. Next step: a callback, or your next scheduled touch. Keep it under 20 seconds.
● SCRIPT · NEW LEAD BY TEXT
"Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Your Brokerage]. Thanks for your interest in [property or area]. Happy to help. What's the best time for a quick call today?"
Purpose: get a reply. Next step: a time for a call. If they answer with a question, answer it and ask one back. Send this as a personal, non-automated text unless you have recorded their consent to text (Chapters 4 and 11).
● SCRIPT · SPHERE ANNOUNCEMENT
"Hi [Name], it's [Your Name]. I wanted you to hear it from me first: I've just started as a real estate agent with [Brokerage]. If you or anyone you know is thinking about buying or selling, I'd be glad to help. And I'd love to hear what you've been up to. How are you?"
Purpose: tell people, warmly, without asking for business. Next step: a real conversation. Log it as a conversation, not a pitch.
● SCRIPT · SPHERE REFERRAL ASK
"I'm building my business on introductions from people I trust, and I'd be grateful if you thought of me when someone you know mentions buying or selling. No pressure."
Purpose: an introduction. Use it after you have given two or three helpful touches (Chapter 2). Next step: a name and permission to reach out, or "I'll keep you in mind." Either is a win. Thank them and keep in touch.
● SCRIPT · OPEN HOUSE GREETING
"Welcome in, I'm [Your Name]. Please sign in on the tablet. What brings you out today, are you looking in this neighborhood or just curious about this one?"
Purpose: greet and ask one open question. Next step: their name, phone and email, and one concrete follow-up offer.
● SCRIPT · EXPIRED LISTING OR FSBO (ADVANCED)
"Hi [Name], I'm [Your Name] with [Your Brokerage]. I saw your home is no longer listed. What would you like to happen next?"
Purpose: lead with curiosity, not criticism. Next step: listen, then offer a short conversation. Postpone this source until your sphere and follow-up are working.
● SCRIPT · BUYER CONSULTATION OPENING
"My goal today is to understand exactly what you're looking for, walk you through how the process works, and make sure you feel confident about the steps ahead. Does that work for you?"
Chapter 9 teaches the full consultation. Purpose: set the frame. Next step: their agreement, then your questions. Cover timeline, motivation, financing and must-haves before you talk about homes.
● SCRIPT · SELLER CONSULTATION OPENING
"I'd like to learn about your goals and timeline first. Then I'll show you what similar homes have done and how I'd market yours. By the end you should have a clear picture of whether I'm the right fit."
Chapter 9 teaches the full consultation. Purpose: earn the right to present. Next step: their goals, then your comparables, then your marketing plan, then the listing agreement.
● SCRIPT · OBJECTION: "I'M JUST LOOKING."
"That's completely fine. Most people start that way. Would it help if I sent you a few homes that match what you described, so you can see what's out there? No pressure either way."
Chapter 9 covers objections in depth. Purpose: keep the door open and earn permission to stay in touch. Next step: their email and a follow-up plan.
● SCRIPT · POST-CLOSE REFERRAL REQUEST
"It's been a pleasure working with you. If you know anyone else who might be thinking about a move, I'd be grateful for the introduction. I'll take great care of them."
Purpose: turn a happy client into a source. Next step: a name, or a review on your Agent Site. Ask at the closing table and again at the 30-day check-in.
● TRY IT TODAY
• Rewrite the new-lead phone script in your own words. Use the lead from your Chapter 5 speed-to-lead test as the person you are calling. Record yourself three times, and score each recording: did I (1) use their name and the thing they asked about, (2) end on one question, (3) stop talking after it? Aim for three yes.
• Role-play the buyer consultation opening with a colleague. Swap roles. Give your partner the same three-item checklist so feedback is specific.
• Use the sphere referral ask on three people from your Chapter 2 list who have already received a "give" touch. Log the outcomes in your CRM.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. A script succeeds when it reaches its next step, not when you say it word for word.
  2. Be specific, ask one question, then be quiet.
  3. Practice out loud and on recording. Reading is not rehearsal.
