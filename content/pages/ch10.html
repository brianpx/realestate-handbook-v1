PART VI: Serve and Keep

10. Keeping clients: long-term relationships

A closing feels like the end. For the client it is the day the move is real; for you it should be the day the relationship properly begins.

The people you serve today are the ones most likely to call you, and to send you their friends, in the years ahead. They will not do that because you were pleasant at the closing table. They will do it because you were still there in month three, on their first anniversary, and when a pipe burst in the second winter. A database of past clients and sphere contacts who hear from you regularly is the most valuable asset you will build, and the cheapest one to maintain. This chapter makes "still being there" a schedule instead of a hope.

In this chapter you will learn to

  • Schedule the six-step post-close plan from the day of closing (seven dated touches in the first year), and explain the purpose of each step.
  • Apply one test to any message to decide whether it adds value or only markets.
  • Reactivate a quiet part of your database in batches, starting with one useful message.

The post-close plan

Most homeowners move again within a decade or two, and know several people who will move before then. The agent they remember gets those transactions. Being remembered is a schedule, not an accident.

Table 10.1. The post-close plan: the first year of the relationship. Build it once as a follow-up plan and apply it at every closing. It has six steps. "Quarterly" repeats at months 3, 6 and 9, and the home anniversary at month 12 takes the place of a fourth quarter, so a client gets seven dated touches in the first year. The annual appreciation event or gift is yours to schedule once a year, not a separate dated touch per client. After the first year, the client stays on the Quarterly Call Plan (Appendix B.3).

Adding value without selling

The test for every touch: would the person be glad to receive it if they were never going to move again? Local insights, a reminder to change furnace filters, a list of trusted contractors, a note on a property tax deadline, an invitation to a neighborhood event. These pass the test. "Thinking of selling?" every month does not.

  • Vendor list. Keep a short list of a plumber, electrician, handyman, painter and cleaner you trust. Introducing them is a gift that costs you nothing, as long as you accept nothing of value for it and tell the client they are free to choose anyone (Chapter 11).
  • Home value updates. Once a year, unprompted. Homeowners love knowing. Sellers come from this.
  • Life events. A new baby, a promotion, a graduation. A card beats a market update.

Reactivating a quiet database

If you have let your database go cold, do not apologize for it. Send one useful piece of market information to everyone, then start calling in small batches with a simple "thinking of you" and one question about how things are. Most people will be glad to hear from you. Put everyone you reach back on the quarterly plan so it does not go cold again. A workable pace is a batch of 10 a day, starting with the people who liked your last message.

Asking for referrals

Ask at three moments: at closing, at the 30-day call, and any time a client thanks you unprompted. Ask plainly, using the script from Chapter 6, and then stop. Thank every referral within a day, by phone or a handwritten note, whether or not it becomes a transaction. Track each one in your CRM with a "Referred by" field, so you can see how many of your leads come from past clients.

● FIELD NOTE
Track one more number: referrals received per month. When this number grows, your business becomes less dependent on paid leads every year. Many experienced agents report that most of their business now comes from past clients and referrals.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. Every closed client goes on a post-close plan the day of closing.
  2. Give value they would want even if they never moved again.
  3. Home anniversaries and value updates are where seller conversations begin.
