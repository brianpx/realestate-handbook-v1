# Chapter 10. Keeping clients: long-term relationships

**Verdict.** A short, warm, useful chapter with a memorable test for every touch ("would they be glad to receive it if they were never going to move again?"). It closes the loop opened by the preface's Figure 1. The weakness is audience fit: its exercises and several claims assume the reader already has past clients, while this handbook is written for an agent in their first 90 days with none. There is also no script for any of the six touches, no example of a "value" message, and no diagram of the referral loop the preface promises.

## Title vs. content

"Keeping clients: long-term relationships" matches: a post-close plan, adding value, and reactivating a database. Two observations:

- **"Keeping" is passive.** What the chapter really teaches is *staying useful so they send the next ones.* The chapter lede says this ("the most valuable asset you will build"). A title like "Keeping clients and earning referrals" would align with the Key Takeaways and the closing Field Note.
- **Referrals are not taught.** They appear in the touch table and the Field Note. The referral *ask* is in Chapter 6 (a script) and Chapter 10 (a table cell). A reader has no section that says: when to ask, how often, how to thank, how to track. Add 150 words.

Rewrite:

> In this chapter you will learn to:
> - **Schedule** six post-close touches from the day of closing, and **explain** the purpose of each.
> - **Apply** one test to any message to decide whether it adds value or only markets.
> - **Reactivate** a quiet part of your database in batches, starting with one useful message.

## Opening and transition

There is no part intro; Chapter 9 closes with "After the transaction. Stay in touch… Chapter 10 shows the post-close plan." That is the bridge, and the chapter should pick it up directly.

**Replacement opener:**

> A closing feels like the end. For the client it is the day the move is real; for you it should be the day the relationship properly begins.
>
> The people you serve today are the ones most likely to call you, and to send you their friends, in five or ten years. They will not do that because you were pleasant at the closing table. They will do it because you were still there in month three, on their first anniversary, and when a pipe burst in the second winter. This chapter makes "still being there" a schedule instead of a hope.

(Check the "five or ten years" wording against the sourced figure. See Prose.)

## Prose and accuracy

- **"The average homeowner moves again within seven to ten years."** Unsourced and probably out of date: reported median tenure in the same home has been rising, and may now exceed ten years. Check against the most recent NAR figure and cite it; or write "Most homeowners move again within a decade or two, and know people who will move before then."
- **"Agents in their fifth year often get more than half their business this way."** Unsourced. Replace with "Many experienced agents report that most of their business now comes from past clients and referrals", or cite.
- **"Being remembered is a schedule, not an accident."** An excellent line. Keep, and use in the opener or Key Takeaway.
- **"Thinking of selling?" every month does not** (pass the test). Sharp, memorable. Keep.
- **"Home anniversary: card or call with an updated home value. The most natural seller conversation you will ever have."** Good; add a caution: "An automated estimate is not a market analysis. Say so." That also avoids implying a formal valuation.
- **"Closing day: handwritten note and a small closing gift."** Add: "Check your brokerage's policy on gifts and keep within it." Cheap to add, protective.
- **Vendor list.** "Introducing them is a gift that costs you nothing." True, with one condition: do not accept payment or something of value for recommending vendors, and when you recommend a lender, title or inspection company, tell the client they are free to choose. Chapter 11's documentation list says "client chose freely"; add one sentence here with a pointer. Same issue in Chapter 13, "A good lender … sends you referrals".
- **"Reactivating a quiet database … do not apologize"** is good advice. Add one concrete plan: "Batch of 10 a day, starting with the people who liked your last message."
- **Table 10.1 vs. Table 5.1 vs. the scheduler:** "six touches", "4 to 6" and "7 scheduled touches". See README conflict (i). Settle one count.
