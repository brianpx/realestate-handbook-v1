# Chapter 3. Marketing and brand presence

**Verdict.** The sections that exist are sensible: the content calendar (Table 3.1) is realistic, the "just sold" advice is shrewd, and the paid-ads test (the 9 p.m. lead) is the best sentence in the chapter. But the title promises *brand*, and the chapter never defines or builds one. There are no figures. The opening assumes tools (Agent Site, Client App) the reader has not been introduced to. It is also the one chapter in the book with no part intro, no worked example, and an exercise that asks for a different number of posts than its own table.

## Title vs. content

| Title word | Delivered | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing | Content calendar, listing marketing, paid ads | Good coverage of tactics. No mention of advertising rules (broker name on every ad, license number, team-name rules, MLS display rules for listing photos). |
| Brand | Opening sentence only ("local expert … person who will take care of them") | No definition of what a brand is for a new agent, no niche or positioning exercise, no profile checklist (photo, bio, headline), no consistency rule across channels. The word "brand" is used in "brand colors" in Chapter 4 and "custom website" in the pitfall box, but never taught. |
| Presence | Calendar cadence | Implicitly yes. |

Fix: add a short section (about 300 words) titled **"What people should say about you"**. Teach one idea: a brand for a new agent is a promise stated in one sentence plus proof. Prompt the reader to fill the blanks: *"I help [who] [do what] in [where]."* For example: *"I help first-time buyers in Eastside feel confident about their first offer."* Then connect it to the calendar: every post is proof of that sentence.

**For a new agent with no sold homes, "proof" is the problem.** The Field Note recommends a phone video "standing in front of a sold home", and the listing section recommends a "just sold" post. A new agent has neither on day one. Add one line: *"Until you have your own, borrow a colleague's with their permission, or profile a local business, a school or a park. Credibility starts with knowing the place."*

Rewrite:

> In this chapter you will learn to:
> - **Write** one sentence that says who you help and where, and **use** it in your profile, bio and first post.
> - **Plan** a four-week content calendar you can keep on your busiest week.
> - **List** the five posts every listing should produce, and **explain** how each leads back to you.
> - **Apply** the 9 p.m. test to decide whether you are ready to pay for leads.

## Opening and transition

There is no part intro, and the lede ("Marketing should make people feel two things…") is excellent but arrives without context. Chapter 2 ended on tracking cost per closed deal. Chapter 3 should begin where that leaves the reader: *they now know where leads come from, and the question is why anyone should call them.*

**Replacement opener:**

> In Chapter 2 you chose where your first conversations will come from. Chapter 3 is about what people see once you have started them. A friend you called last week will look you up. A neighbor who picks up your mailer will search your name. Before they decide whether to call you, they will decide who you are.
>
> Marketing should make them feel two things: you are the local expert, and you are the person who will take care of them. Consistency beats perfection. This chapter shows you how to be easy to find, easy to remember, and easy to trust, without spending money you do not yet need to spend.

## Prose and accuracy

- **"Check that every link on your social profiles and email signature points to them."** "Them" refers to the Agent Site and Client App two sentences earlier; the antecedent is too far. Name the thing: "points to your Agent Site."
- **"Phone photos cost sellers money and cost you credibility."** Clear and strong. Keep.
- **"People hire people."** Used as a closing line in the Field Note. Fine, but the previous sentence says "outperforms a polished stock graphic" without evidence. Soften: "tends to outperform".
- **"The test is simple: if a new lead arrived at 9 p.m. tonight…"** The best passage in the chapter. Chapter 4's Field Note uses 8 p.m. for the same test; use one hour. The test assumes the reader has the CRM and plans of Chapters 4–5, which the reader has not yet met. Say so ("You will build all three in Chapters 4 and 5. Until you have, hold off.") rather than leaving the reader with a test they cannot pass.
- **Pitfall box** ("Your first marketing budget is your calendar.") is superb. Quote it in the opener.
- **Advertising rules.** Nothing about required disclosures in ads (brokerage name, license number, Fair Housing language). Add one sentence and a pointer to Chapter 11: "Your broker has rules for what must appear on every ad. Ask before you post."
- **Vendor terms.** "Agent Site" and "Client App" are proper nouns from a platform; define on first use or use generic terms.
- **"Brand" is used but never defined** ("branding" in the pitfall box, "branded search" in the home-base paragraph). Either teach it or retitle to "Marketing and visibility".
