# Chapter 4. Technology and systems

**Verdict.** The content is the book's operating core and is clearly written: a six-step setup, a daily rhythm table, and a two-layer speed-to-lead standard. The title is too wide for what is taught (this is a CRM chapter, not a technology chapter). The two figures are not diagrams. The setup steps depend on plans the reader meets only in Chapter 5. One claim (the response-time window) is presented as data with no numbers or source.

## Title vs. content

The chapter teaches: CRM setup, daily rhythm in the CRM, speed-to-lead, and a paragraph on optional tools. "Technology" would also cover transaction management, e-signature, showing software, scheduling, the MLS and phone etiquette; "systems" implies more than one. None of that is here, and it need not be.

Retitle to **"Your CRM and daily operating system"** (or "Your operating system", matching the part label). The part label "Part III: Your Operating System" already says it. A title that promises less and delivers it is better teaching than one that promises more.

Recommended split:

- **Chapter 4** owns the *day*: Table 4.1 (blocks within a day).
- **Chapter 8** owns the *week*: Table 8.1 (blocks across a week) and drops its "A sample day" list.
- **Chapter 12** owns the *90 days*.

Rewrite:

> In this chapter you will learn to:
> - **Complete** the six-part CRM setup, in order, in your first week.
> - **Run** your day in four blocks (review, follow-up, prospecting, close-out) with a fixed time budget for each.
> - **Set up** a two-layer response to every online inquiry, and **test** it with a friend.
> - **Decide** whether a new tool is worth adding by applying two conditions.

## Opening and transition

Current part intro: "One tool, set up once, used every day…" Good. The lede ("Your CRM is your core tool…") is fine. The bridge from Part II is missing, and it matters: Chapters 2 and 3 end by telling the reader they need a working system before spending money. This chapter is that system.

**Replacement opener:**

> By now you know where leads come from and how people will find you. What you do not yet have is a place to put them. A name in your phone, a number on a sticky note and an email you meant to answer are three different places a lead can disappear.
>
> This chapter gives you one place. You will set up your CRM in your first week, build a daily rhythm around it, and learn the single rule that decides whether online leads are worth paying for: answer within minutes. Chapters 2 and 3 told you to build this before you spend. Here is how.

## Prose and accuracy

- **"Follow Up Boss, branded client apps, AI texting assistants and dialers"** is the only product named in the book. Either name the book's actual CRM wherever it is used, or remove the brand. Mixed naming leaves the reader unsure whether Appendix C applies to their system.
- **"Your CRM rewards agents who set it up completely and punishes those who set it up halfway."** A good sentence; keep.
- **Setup step 3 ("Create or download follow-up plans. Start with an 8x8…")** names four plans, one of which (8x8) is explained for the first time in Table 5.1. The reader meets "8x8" cold. Add "(an eight-week plan for new sphere contacts; Chapter 5 explains it)".
- **Two-layer standard, layer 1.** "Automated first touch, within one minute… a text and an email" and then "Get permission before an automated marketing text." The compliance caveat arrives last, after the reader has the instruction. For a new agent the cautious order is: email always, text only with permission. Reorder so the rule is a condition of the step, not an afterthought. See README #5.
- **"Response time is the single biggest factor you control in online-lead conversion."** A strong claim with no source. Soften to "among the biggest factors".
- **"A new agent with a working CRM setup and 30 leads a month does not need a second CRM."** Nice and specific. Keep.
- **Table 4.1** says "Protect the first three blocks before you accept any appointment." The Chapter 1 Try It blocks a different two-hour window. Make one rule across the book.
