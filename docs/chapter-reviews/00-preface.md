# Preface: How to use this handbook

**Verdict.** The preface has the right idea, the three-outcome system of Find, Nurture and Keep, and states it cleanly. It does not yet do the job of a preface for a training text: it never tells the reader who the book is for, what they can reasonably have in place after the first month and by day 90, or how a chapter is built. The reading plan differs from the book order without saying why, and the "under an hour" promise on exercises is not true for several chapters.

Priorities, in order: audience, realistic outcomes, the four-week versus 90-day distinction, early prerequisites, manageable exercises. Chapter reordering and elaborate graphics are deferred until those are settled.

## Title vs. content

The cover and preface call it a *Training Manual*, "Finding, Keeping, and Nurturing Clients". Everything in the book serves those three verbs, so the title is honest. One real mismatch:

- The title order is **Find, Keep, Nurture**; the book order and the Figure 1 order are **Find, Nurture, Keep**. Pick one. The lifecycle order is the right one.

"Handbook", "manual" and "book" can coexist naturally in running prose. Consistency matters in the product title and navigation, not everywhere.

## Learning objectives

The preface has no book-level objectives. A learner needs the end state before the first chapter. Suggested box, placed under the first paragraph, with outcomes that are observable and do not depend on opportunities the learner may not get:

> **By day 90 you will have:**
> - a written income goal turned into a number of conversations you hold each day;
> - a CRM that shows you every morning who to call, with a recorded next action on every active lead;
> - your follow-up plans running (see the note below on how many);
> - a buyer consultation and a seller consultation rehearsed, and live consultations as they come;
> - a pipeline you review every Friday, and a post-close plan ready for the day your first client closes.

Notes on the wording:

- "A first set of consultations behind you" depends on leads the learner may not receive. "Rehearsed" is the baseline; live consultations are an application goal.
- "No lead goes quiet unnoticed" is an absolute promise. "Every active lead has a recorded next action" is measurable.
- Reconcile the plan count. The earlier drafts say "three follow-up plans" but Chapter 4 names four (8x8, QCP, new-lead, and one more). Use one number everywhere.

Chapter objectives should map to these outcomes where natural. They need not map to exactly one: compliance and professional judgment (Ch. 11) support all five, and Chapter 13 can work as a synthesis without the standard teaching template.

## Opening and transition

The first paragraph ("Most new agents do not fail for lack of effort…") reads well but states a broad causal claim. Qualify it: "When you're starting out, effort can become scattered across activities that do not compound."

What follows moves directly to the three outcomes without saying who the reader is or what they need before starting.

**Add, before "How to use this handbook"** (about 120 words):

> This handbook is for the agent in their first year: licensed, working with a brokerage, and unsure what to do on a Tuesday morning when no one is calling. It does not teach you to write a contract or run a closing. Your broker and your state course do that. It teaches the other half of the job: how to find people who might buy or sell, stay in touch until they are ready, and earn the next introduction when they close.
>
> You will not read it once and finish. You will work it, a chapter at a time.

Drop "the part no exam tests"; it is unnecessary and may be inaccurate. Confirm the intended audience (README Decision B) before publishing "first-year, licensed, brokerage-affiliated" as the scope.

**Four weeks versus ninety days.** "Over about ninety days" sits beside a four-week reading plan and reads as a contradiction. State the relationship directly:

> Read the book and do the first exercise from each chapter over four weeks. Then keep building and practicing through day 90.

Separate three kinds of time: reading time, one-off setup time (CRM, import, plans), and ongoing habits (daily block, Friday review).

**Reading plan.** The plan is Week 1: Ch. 1, 4, 12; Week 2: Ch. 2, 5, 6; Week 3: Ch. 7, 8, 9; Week 4: Ch. 3, 10, 11, 13. A reading plan may legitimately differ from chapter order (install the system first, then learn what to measure). The real problems are:

- Chapter 11's consent, broker-approved outreach and Fair Housing rules come in Week 4, but Week 2's activities (texting leads, open houses) need them.
- Chapter 2 points to Chapter 3 for when paid lead generation makes sense, yet Chapter 3 is read after Chapters 7–9.
- Bridges between chapters are false for anyone following the plan.

Recommendation: add a short **"Before you contact anyone"** section to the preface (broker-approved outreach, consent for calls and texts, Fair Housing basics, with a pointer to Chapter 11), and place the full chapter where it best fits. Fix cross-references and transitions first; consider reordering only if that proves insufficient (README Decision A).

## Exercises

The preface says the exercise is "under an hour". Several are not, and the figures below are estimates to validate with a few new agents, not facts. CRM access, size of the existing contact list and import cleanup can change them a lot.

| Chapter | Task | Estimate (to validate) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Build a 150-name sphere list and import it | 2–3 hours |
| 4 | Finish the full CRM checklist (Appendix C) | Up to a day |
| 8 | Build 12 weeks of recurring blocks | 30–45 min |
| 10 | Build the post-close plan in the CRM | 1–2 hours |

Split the larger ones into a **first-session task** (for example, add your first 20 contacts and one follow-up plan) and an **extended setup task** (full import, remaining plans). "An exercise for the same day" overpromises for a full CRM installation. Suggested legend: "An exercise to start the same day. Some take an hour; CRM setup takes longer and is split in two."

The legend should also cover boxes that exist but are not described: the **Read this first** legal box (Ch. 11), **Key takeaways**, and the **Test your knowledge** quiz.

## Diagrams

**Figure 1 (lifecycle)** is a text box with arrows. Keep it useful but make it a loop, with three arrows only: Find → Nurture → Keep → Find, the last labelled "Referrals". The earlier prompt drew three connecting arrows plus a fourth return arrow, which duplicates the loop.

*Prompt: Figure 1, redrawn.*

> [Shared style from README.] Title: "Figure 1. The client lifecycle." Draw a closed loop of three large rounded rectangles arranged clockwise: **1 Find**, **2 Nurture**, **3 Keep** (all blue). Exactly three curved arrows join them: Find → Nurture, Nurture → Keep, and Keep → Find, the last labelled "Referrals". Under each rectangle, two short lines: Find: "Sphere, open houses, online leads, farming". Nurture: "Calls, texts, emails over 90 days". Keep: "Post-close plan, anniversary, referrals". In the centre, a small circle labelled "Your CRM". Caption: "Every chapter serves one of these three jobs."

**Book map: use a table, not a graphic.** A seven-column diagram with overlapping Find/Nurture/Keep brackets is hard to read on a phone and blurs the categories. A compact part / chapter / action table does the job:

| Part | Chapters | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| I Foundations | 1 | Set a number |
| II Find | 2–3 | Start conversations |
| III Your Operating System | 4 | Set up your CRM |
| IV Nurture | 5–6 | Follow up and say the words |
| V Stay on Track | 7–8 | Avoid errors, keep a rhythm |
| VI Serve and Keep | 9–11 | Consult, keep clients, stay compliant |
| VII Execute | 12–13 | Run the plan, find resources |

Appendices A–F: scorecard, plan kits, checklists, glossary, tools, links.

## Prose and accuracy

- Opening sentence: qualify as above ("When you're starting out, effort can become scattered…"). The rest of the paragraph can stay.
- "Each chapter opens with what you will be able to do by the end, teaches the idea, shows the words to say, and closes with an exercise and a checkpoint." Accurate for most chapters. Chapter 13 is a synthesis and Chapter 11 has no scripts. Say "most chapters", or fix the chapters.
- "Do not try to install everything in one weekend." Good, concrete. Follow with the *how*: "Do the Try It for the chapter you just read, and stop."
- "Opportunity" (capitalised) first appears in Chapter 1 undefined. Define it where first used, and add it to Appendix D. If it names a specific CRM feature, say so.
- The overview video is not summarized in text. A two-sentence summary under the video helps readers who cannot play it.

**Disposition.** Accept with revisions: audience, realistic outcomes, four-week versus 90-day distinction, early prerequisites, manageable exercises first.
