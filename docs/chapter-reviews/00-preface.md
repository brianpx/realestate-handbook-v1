# Preface: How to use this handbook

**Verdict.** The preface has the right idea, the three-outcome system of Find, Nurture and Keep, and states it cleanly. It does not yet do the job of a preface for a training text: it never tells the reader who the book is for, what they will have achieved in 90 days, or how a chapter is built. The reading plan contradicts the book order. The "under an hour" promise on exercises is not true for several chapters.

## Title vs. content

The cover and preface call it a *Training Manual*, "Finding, Keeping, and Nurturing Clients". Everything in the book serves those three verbs, so the title is honest. Two small mismatches:

- The title order is **Find, Keep, Nurture**; the book order and the Figure 1 order are **Find, Nurture, Keep**. Pick one. The lifecycle order is the right one.
- The preface alternates "handbook", "manual" and "book". Use one.

## Learning objectives

The preface has no book-level objectives. A learner needs the end state before the first chapter. Suggested box, placed under the first paragraph:

> **By day 90 you will have:**
> - a written income goal turned into a number of conversations you hold each day;
> - a CRM that tells you every morning who to call;
> - three follow-up plans running, so no lead goes quiet unnoticed;
> - a first set of consultations behind you, and a pipeline you review every Friday;
> - a post-close plan ready for the day your first client closes.

Every chapter objective in the book should map to one of those five outcomes. That mapping is the book's table of contents for learning.

## Opening and transition

The first paragraph ("Most new agents do not fail for lack of effort…") is the best sentence in the preface. Keep it. What follows moves directly to the three outcomes without saying who the reader is or what they need before starting.

**Add, before "How to use this handbook"** (about 120 words):

> This handbook is for the agent in their first year: licensed, affiliated with a brokerage, and unsure what to do on a Tuesday morning when no one is calling. It does not teach you to write a contract or run a closing. Your broker and your state course do that. It teaches the other half of the job, the part no exam tests: how to find people who might buy or sell, stay in touch until they are ready, and earn the next introduction when they close.
>
> You will not read it once and finish. You will work it, a chapter at a time, over about ninety days.

Decide who the audience is (see README Decision B) before finalizing; "affiliated with a brokerage" is the general-market wording.

**Reading plan.** The plan is Week 1: Ch. 1, 4, 12; Week 2: Ch. 2, 5, 6; Week 3: Ch. 7, 8, 9; Week 4: Ch. 3, 10, 11, 13. The reasoning is sound for Week 1 (install the system, then learn what to measure) but:

- It places Chapter 3 (marketing) after Chapters 7–9, while Chapter 2 sends the reader to Chapter 3 for "when paid lead generation makes sense".
- Chapter 11's consent and Fair Housing rules come in Week 4. Week 2's activities (texting leads, open houses) need them.
- It makes chapter-to-chapter bridges false for anyone following it.

Recommendation: reorder the book to match the plan's logic (system first), or reorder the plan to match the book. See README Decision A.

## Exercises

The preface says the exercise is "under an hour". Several are not:

| Chapter | Task | Realistic time |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Build a 150-name sphere list and import it | 2–3 hours |
| 4 | Finish the full CRM checklist (Appendix C) | A day |
| 8 | Build 12 weeks of recurring blocks | 30–45 min, fine |
| 10 | Build the post-close plan in the CRM | 1–2 hours |

Rephrase the box legend to "An exercise for the same day. Some take an hour; the CRM setup takes longer." Honest time estimates matter for adult learners deciding whether to start.

The legend should also cover the boxes that exist but are not described: the **Read this first** legal box (Ch. 11), **Key takeaways**, and the **Test your knowledge** quiz.

**Missing: a book map.** The preface lists the seven parts nowhere. A reader cannot see how 13 chapters group into Foundations, Find, Operating System, Nurture, Stay on Track, Serve and Keep, Execute.

## Prose and accuracy

- "Most new agents do not fail for lack of effort. They fail because effort is scattered across activities that do not compound." Excellent. Keep, unchanged.
- "Each chapter opens with what you will be able to do by the end, teaches the idea, shows the words to say, and closes with an exercise and a checkpoint." Accurate for most chapters. Chapter 13 has neither objectives, exercise nor checkpoint; Chapter 11 has no scripts. Either adjust the sentence or fix the chapters.
- "Do not try to install everything in one weekend." Good, concrete. Follow with the *how*: "Do the Try It for the chapter you just read, and stop."
- The word "Opportunities" first appears (capitalised) in Chapter 1 without a definition. The glossary dotted-term feature will handle this for terms; add "Opportunity" to Appendix D.
- The overview video is not summarized in text. A two-sentence summary under the video helps readers who cannot play it.
