# Chapter 12. Daily, weekly and monthly execution plans

**Verdict.** This should be the book's capstone: the chapter where everything the reader has learned becomes one dated plan. It is close. The 30/60/90 structure is right, the milestones are concrete, and the checklist figure is useful. But it does not synthesize: it points back to earlier chapters rather than assembling them. The title promises *daily* plans and the chapter has none. An objective says "four numbers" and the chapter lists thirteen. And the reader never makes anything new here, so the cumulative work of the book does not culminate.

## Title vs. content

The title lists three timescales (daily, weekly, monthly). The chapter delivers:

| Scale | In chapter 12? |
|---|---|
| Daily | **No.** The daily rhythm is in Ch. 4 and Ch. 8. No daily content here. |
| Weekly | Yes: "Measure every week" (a pointer to Appendix A). |
| Monthly | Yes: "Review every month" (four questions). |
| 90 days | Yes: milestone roadmap and three phase descriptions. This is the real subject. |

Retitle to **"Your first 90 days"** (Part VII is "Execute"). Or add a half-page "A day in week 6" that shows the Ch. 4 daily rhythm with real numbers filled in, so the title is true. Prefer the retitle plus a short "daily" recap box ("Your day, in one line: open the CRM, clear follow-ups, prospect to your number, appointments, close the CRM").

> - **Assemble** your 90-Day Business Plan from the work you did in Chapters 1 to 11.

## Opening and transition

The part intro is excellent: "The first 90 days, measured weekly. Everything in this book reduces to a few numbers you can check every Friday." The chapter lede ("Ninety days is long enough…") is also strong. What's missing is the sense of arrival: the reader has covered eleven chapters.

**Replacement opener:**

> You have now met every part of the system. You know where your first conversations come from, where they are recorded, how you follow up, what you say, how you spend your mornings, how you run a consultation, and how you stay in touch after a closing. What remains is to put dates on it.
>
> Ninety days is long enough to build the system and start a pipeline, and short enough to hold yourself to. This chapter turns the book into a calendar: three phases of thirty days, one scorecard every Friday, and one hour each month to decide where your time goes next. When you finish it, you will have a plan with your name on it.

## Prose and accuracy

- **"Do not worry about closings; worry about whether the system is on."** One of the most useful sentences in the book. Use it as the heading for Days 1–30.
- **"By day 60 you should have held your first consultations and signed your first buyer representation agreement before you tour."** Awkward, because "before you tour" dangles. Write: "…and signed a written buyer representation agreement before touring any homes."
- **The compliance repetition** ("Chapter 11 covers that written agreement, and that compensation is negotiable and is no longer an MLS field. Confirm both with your broker") is duplicated from Chapters 9 and 11. State it once here: "Chapter 11 covers buyer agreements."
- **"Measure every week" bullets** compress thirteen rows into four lines. A reader who ticks off "outbound calls, conversations, …" will not know the targets without flipping to Table 8.2. Add the five core numbers inline.
- **"Closings usually follow in the second 90 days."** Honest and reassuring; good counterweight to the discouragement loop in Chapter 7. Quote it in the opener.
- **Monthly review**: four questions are excellent. "Write the answers in your CRM as a note to yourself" is a good small habit; call it a "monthly note" so it is findable.
- **Days 61–90 bullet** "Count the results by lead source and decide, with data, whether paid leads make sense next quarter" refers back to Chapters 2–3's test. Link it: "using the 9 p.m. test from Chapter 3."
