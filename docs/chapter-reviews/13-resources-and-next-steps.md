# Chapter 13. Resources and next steps

**Verdict.** The shortest chapter and the only one with none of the book's teaching scaffolding: no learning objectives, no Try It, no Checkpoint, no figure. The "whole handbook on one card" is a genuinely good device. But the chapter reads as a list of reminders rather than a close: it does not look back at what the reader has done, does not look ahead to what comes after day 90, and the "Resources" in its title are mostly "ask your office". The final two habits and the seven-line card also disagree on what matters most.

## Title vs. content

| Title phrase | Delivered | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Resources | "Where to get help" (office, brokerage training, broker, lender partners) | No actual resources: no reading, no tools, no associations. Appendix F ("Essential Links") exists; cross-reference it. |
| Next steps | "Do these this week" (five items) | Covers the next seven days only. After day 90 there is nothing: no next-90-days plan, no advice on using the conversion ratios the reader has recorded, no pointer to a second-year agenda (listings, a niche, a team, a coach). |

Retitle to **"Where you go from here"**, or keep the title and restructure into three parts: *Look back* (what you can now do), *Do this week* (existing list), *Plan the next 90 days* (new).

## Learning objectives

None. For a closing chapter, objectives are still useful: they frame the reflection. Suggested:

> In this chapter you will learn to:
> - **Review** your first 90 days against the scorecard and **identify** what worked.
> - **Set** the next 90 days' targets from your own conversion ratios, not the book's examples.
> - **Choose** the mentor, training and professional contacts you will use in your first year.

## Opening and transition

The chapter begins "This handbook is a starting point." True and modest, but it opens a closing chapter with an apology. After Chapter 12's capstone, the reader has a plan; this chapter should honor that and look forward.

**Replacement opener:**

> You began with a goal and a question: what do I do on a Tuesday morning? You now have a number to hit, a place to record it, a plan for every lead, words to say, a calendar to protect, and a way to stay in touch after the closing. That is a business, and it is yours.
>
> This handbook is a starting point. The next ninety days are where it becomes real, and the ninety after that are where you replace the book's examples with your own numbers. The pages that follow give you the week ahead, the people to lean on, and the two habits that matter more than everything else.

## Exercises

No exercise today. A closing chapter is the best place for a **final integrative task** and a **commitment device**. Suggested, building on Chapter 12's capstone:

1. **Write a one-paragraph review of your first 90 days** (or, if you are still reading, of the first 30): what moved, what did not, what surprised you. Put it in the CRM as a note dated day 90.
2. **Replace three of the book's example numbers with your own.** Conversations per appointment (Ch. 1), conversion by source (Ch. 2), touches before a reply (Ch. 5). Write the new ratio next to the old one.
3. **Choose one person** to share the review with: your broker, a mentor or an accountability partner (Ch. 8). Put a date on it.
4. **Implementation intention:** *"On [day], at [time], I will [action]."* Write one for next Monday morning.

**Final knowledge check.** The book ends with 13 chapter-level quizzes but no cumulative assessment. Add a short *whole-book check* (five questions, one from each Part) so the reader has a retrieval-practice finish. Spaced retrieval across Parts is among the most reliable ways to improve long-term retention.

## Prose and accuracy

- **"The two habits that matter most"** names speed-to-lead and multi-touch follow-up, but the card above it lists seven, and the Key Takeaways of earlier chapters give priority to others (leading indicators, the CRM). A reader will ask: which two, which seven? Reconcile by saying the seven are the system and the two are the first things to check when results stall. Better still, put the two in one sentence before the card, as the card's first two items.
- **"Everything else in the book exists to make them easy."** Strong and clear. Keep.
- **"Ask your office for follow-up plan recommendations and the local training calendar."** Generic. The reader may not have an "office" in the brokerage sense; see README #8.
- **"A good lender teaches you financing and sends you referrals. Meet three and pick one."** A referral relationship with a lender raises real estate settlement rules in many jurisdictions (compensation or things of value for referrals). Add: "Ask your broker what is and is not allowed before you agree to anything." See [11](11-legal-and-professional-standards.md).
- **"Pick one chapter, do its exercise, and move to the next."** The best advice in the chapter and the closest thing to a next step. Move it first.
- **Appendix cross-references**: the chapter never mentions Appendix F (Essential Links) or Appendix E (interactive tools). Point to both. A "Resources" chapter that does not link the resources is the clearest fix on this page.
- **Missing sign-off.** After 13 chapters the voice falls away. One sentence of encouragement in the book's own tone ("Nobody will assign you a task list on Monday morning; now you have written your own") would close the loop with Chapter 1.
