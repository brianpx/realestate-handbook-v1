# Chapter 2. Finding clients

**Verdict.** The most practical chapter in Part II. It teaches five lead sources in a sensible order (sphere first), gives a four-step sphere method, and the open-house before/during/after structure is a model of how to teach a procedure. Three problems: the chapter sends the reader out to do things whose words and rules come later (the announcement message, texting, qualifying questions); one objective is undercut by the chapter's own caption; and the exercises start from zero rather than from the Chapter 1 number the reader just calculated.

## Title vs. content

"Finding clients" is delivered: sphere, online leads, open houses, farming, expireds/FSBOs, tracking. Two observations:

- The title says *clients*, the content describes *leads and conversations*. That is the right distinction for a new agent, but say it once: "You are not finding clients yet. You are finding conversations that become clients."
- Part II contains Chapters 2 and 3, but Chapter 3 (marketing) is the *how you are seen* half of Find. Chapter 2 should end with a sentence handing off to it.

Not covered, and arguably in scope: **past-client and referral partners** (lenders, attorneys, other agents, relocation contacts) as a source. Chapter 10 handles past clients, but a new agent has none; referral partners are the next-best source after the sphere and are absent from Figure 2.

## Learning objectives

| 2 | Compare lead sources by conversion rate, cost and exclusivity. | Figure 2 | None | None | Figure 2 says its ranges are "illustrative … not comparable across sources". The objective asks the reader to compare them. |
| 3 | Run an open house as a lead-generation event rather than a waiting room. | Yes, thoroughly | Try It 3 (schedule one) | Checkpoint 3 | Aligned. |
| 4 | Track cost per closed deal by source. | One paragraph | None | Key Takeaway only | Under-taught: no formula, no worked example, no exercise. |

Rewrite:

> In this chapter you will learn to:
> - **List** at least 150 people in your sphere and **contact** them in the order announce, give, ask, repeat.
> - **Choose** the first two lead sources to work, based on what each costs in time and money and what you can follow up on today.
> - **Run** an open house in three phases (before, during, after) so that every visitor enters a follow-up plan.
> - **Calculate** cost per closed deal for one source from four monthly counts.

Objective 4 needs a **worked example** in the chapter. For instance: *Open houses, month one. 4 houses, 38 visitors captured, 6 appointments, 1 closing in month 5. Cost: $120 materials + 24 hours at $25 an hour = $720. Cost per closed deal: $720 ÷ 1 = $720.* That makes the abstract formula usable.

## Opening and transition

Current part intro: "Where clients come from, what each source costs, and how to make your name mean 'local expert' before anyone needs you." Current lede: "Every lead source trades time for money or money for time." The lede is the best sentence in the chapter. The missing piece is the bridge to Chapter 1.

**Replacement opener:**

> In Chapter 1 you worked out that your income goal needs roughly 16 conversations a week. That is a number. It is not yet a plan, because the next question is the practical one: *with whom?*
>
> Every lead source trades time for money or money for time. Some cost nothing but nerve; some cost a monthly bill. This chapter shows you the five sources new agents use, what each demands of you, and how to start with the one that is cheapest and works best, the people who already know your name.

Note the placement of "Chapter 1" inside the opener: the bridge is explicit, and it delivers the number the reader will use in the exercise.

> Take your weekly conversation number from Chapter 1. Split it across your sources. A new agent might start with: sphere 10, open house follow-ups 3, other 3. Write the split on your card from Chapter 1.

**Add the missing announcement script** (for a new Script box):

> "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name]. I wanted you to hear it from me first: I've just started as a real estate agent with [Brokerage]. If you or anyone you know is thinking about buying or selling, I'd be glad to help. And I'd love to hear what you've been up to. How are you?"

## Diagrams

**Figure 2 is a table, not a diagram.** The table is fine as a table, but it is labelled and cited as a diagram, and the numbers in it are not comparable (the caption says so). A reader will compare them anyway, because they sit in a column. Options: keep as "Table 2.1" and keep the caption warning, or replace with a **cost-versus-time map** that does not need conversion numbers.

*Prompt: Figure 2.1, the trade-off map (replaces the percent column).*

> [Shared style.] Title: "Figure 2.1. Every lead source trades time for money." A square plot area. Horizontal axis: "Your time (low → high)". Vertical axis: "Your money (low → high)". Place five labelled circles: **Sphere of influence and referrals** (high time, low money), **Open houses** (high time, low money, slightly lower than sphere on time), **Expired listings and FSBOs** (high time, low money), **Own website and ads** (medium time, medium-high money), **Portal leads (Zillow and similar)** (low time, high money). Circle size: all the same; do not imply a rate. Shade the lower-right quadrant (high time, low money) in a very light blue and label it "Where new agents start." Add a small amber note at the bottom: "Conversion rates vary by market and agent. Track your own."

*Prompt: Figure 2.2, the sphere method.*

> [Shared style.] Title: "Figure 2.2. Working your sphere: announce, give, ask, repeat." Four rounded rectangles in a loop (clockwise), numbered 1 to 4: **Announce** ("One personal message, not a blast"), **Give** ("Market update, checklist, an introduction to a contractor"), **Ask** ("After two or three helpful touches, ask for an introduction"), **Repeat** ("Put everyone on a touch plan"). A thin arrow from **Repeat** back to **Give**, not to **Announce**, because you announce only once. Beneath the loop, a horizontal bar labelled "Two to three gives before one ask."

A third diagram is useful if the author keeps the open-house section as three bulleted lists: **Open house timeline** (24 hours before → during → 24 hours after).

*Prompt: Figure 2.3, the open-house timeline.*

> [Shared style.] Title: "Figure 2.3. The open house is a three-day event." A horizontal timeline with three zones. **Before (day before):** "Preview the home and five comparable sales", "Invite the 20 nearest neighbors", "Set up digital sign-in". **During:** "Greet and ask one open question", "Capture name, phone, email", "Offer one concrete next step". **After (within 24 hours):** "Text that evening", "Call next morning", "Put every visitor on a follow-up plan", "Log as a lead source". A thin arrow loops from the last zone to a small box labelled "Cost per closed deal" with the caption "Measure every source this way."

## Prose and accuracy

- **"Your sphere converts at five to ten times the rate of a portal lead."** Stated as fact. Figure 2's own caption says the ranges are not comparable. Either remove the multiplier or write "often converts far better". See README #7.
- **The open-house lists (Before / During / After)** are well structured but give no reason for the sequence. One sentence under each heading ("Before is about being able to answer a price question on the spot; During is about capturing, not selling; After is where the leads are made") would convert a checklist into understanding.
- **"Expect six to twelve months before listings appear" (farming)** is a useful honest expectation. Keep. Add "so farm only if you can afford the wait".
- **"Pick a farm with a turnover rate of at least 6 percent a year"** is stated without a source. Say "a rule of thumb many agents use".
- **Forward references.** The chapter points to Chapter 4 (response window), Chapter 5 (qualifying questions), Chapter 6 (the words) and Chapter 3. A reader in book order has not read any of them. Keep the pointers, but each should be a short parenthetical, not a dependency: "(Chapter 5 teaches the four questions; for now, ask when they hope to move)".
- **Expired/FSBO paragraph** gives no wording or method, only a mood ("lead with curiosity"). It is the thinnest of the five sources. Either add a two-line script or tell the reader it is an advanced source to postpone.
- **Checkpoint 2** ("What has to be true before you pay for online leads?") is answered here with "your follow-up system is built and tested on your sphere". Chapter 3 gives a different, three-part test. Make them one test. See [03](03-marketing-and-brand-presence.md).
