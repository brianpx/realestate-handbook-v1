# Chapter 1. Mindset and business foundations

**Verdict.** The strongest chapter for teaching method: it has a worked example (the goal ladder), a crisp distinction (leading vs. lagging), and a Try It that makes the reader do the arithmetic. The title promises "mindset", though, and the chapter teaches business arithmetic and a CRM habit. The reader's first real obstacle, fear of rejection and the discipline to make calls, is untouched. The checkpoint asks for "three numbers" that the chapter never names.

## Title vs. content

| Title word | What the chapter teaches | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Mindset | One paragraph ("You own a business now"). | No treatment of rejection, call reluctance, identity shift from employee to owner, or how to stay steady through 60–120 days with no closings. That last point is *named* (Ch. 8 returns to it) but not taught here. |
| Business | Goal ladder, leading/lagging indicators. | Solid. Missing: what a *month one* budget looks like (license fees, dues, MLS, CRM, marketing) and the cash-runway reality of a commission business. A new agent does the income math without the cost math. |
| Foundations | CRM as headquarters. | Reasonable, but "Opportunities" and "CRM" are used with no definition. |

Two ways to fix it, either is fine:

- **Keep the title, add the mindset.** Add a short section (about 250 words) after "You own a business now": *The work is the win.* Teach one idea: the daily conversation number is the thing the reader controls, and a "no" still counts toward it. Use a 1-in-N framing: if it takes 20 conversations to book an appointment, then each "no" is 1/20th of one.
- **Retitle** to "Running your practice as a business" and move mindset content to Chapter 8, which already owns motivation.

The first is better. Mindset is the reason people read chapter 1 of a training book.

## Learning objectives

| # | Objective as written | Taught | Practiced | Assessed | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell leading indicators from lagging, and set goals on the right one. | Yes | Not directly (Try It never asks the reader to list indicators) | Checkpoint 1 | Add an exercise. |
| 2 | Work backward from an income goal to a daily number of conversations. | Yes (Table 1.1) | Yes (Try It 1) | Checkpoint 2 | Aligned. |
| 3 | Treat your CRM as your daily headquarters rather than an occasional tool. | One paragraph | Calendar block, no CRM action | Checkpoint 3 | A disposition, not a skill. Rewrite. |

Rewrite (observable verbs, one clause each):

> In this chapter you will learn to:
> - **Sort** a list of activities into leading and lagging indicators, and **choose** three leading indicators to track weekly.
> - **Convert** an annual income goal into the number of conversations you need each day.
> - **Open** your CRM first each morning and **name** the first three things you check there.

**Checkpoint mismatch.** Question 1 asks "Which three numbers will you track every week, and why are none of them closings?" The chapter does not name three. The reader can only guess. Either name them in the leading-indicator section ("conversations, appointments set, follow-ups completed") or change the question to "Pick three leading indicators and explain why you chose them."

## Opening and transition

The existing italic part intro ("Before tactics, the one decision that shapes everything else…") is a theme statement. The chapter's lede ("Real estate is a business, not a job…") is a slogan the reader has met in every real-estate book. Neither tells the reader what changed because they finished the preface.

**Replacement opener** (placed above the learning objectives):

> Nobody hired you to be here. No one sets your Monday schedule, no manager notices when a week goes by without a single real conversation, and no paycheck arrives for effort alone. That freedom is the reason many people come into this business, and the reason many leave it.
>
> This chapter turns the freedom into a plan. You will do one piece of arithmetic that tells you how many conversations a day your income goal requires, and you will learn why that number, not your closings, is the thing to watch. Everything else in the book (finding people, following up, staying in touch after the sale) serves that number.

This keeps the best line from the current chapter ("Nobody will assign you a task list on Monday morning"), moves the stakes to the front, and gives the reader a bridge into the work.

## Exercises

- **Try It 1 (fill in the "Your number" column).** Excellent. It is the chapter's core skill. Keep it. Add: "If you do not yet know your average commission, ask your broker for the figure for an agent in their first year, not a top producer's."
- **Try It 2 (write the target on a card).** Fine, low effort.
- **Try It 3 (block 9:00–11:00 tomorrow).** Conflicts with the rest of the book (Table 8.1 and Chapter 4 use a 90-minute block starting at 9:30). Use one block, one time. See README conflict (b).
- **Missing**: an exercise for objective 1. Add: "List the last five things you did for your business this week. Mark each L (leading) or G (lagging). What share were leading?"
- **How it builds forward.** The daily number from Table 1.1 should be the input to the Chapter 2 exercise (split it across lead sources) and the Chapter 8 scorecard. State that in the Try It: "Keep this number. You will use it in Chapter 2."

Also fix the **ratio conflict**: this chapter's ladder gives about 16 conversations and **0.8 appointments** a week. Table 8.2 asks for 3–6 appointments set. A reader doing this exercise carefully will notice. See README conflict (a).

## Diagrams

**Missing: the goal ladder as a funnel.** Table 1.1 is clear, but the key insight (a big income goal shrinks to a small daily action) is visual. A funnel that narrows from "$80,000" to "3–4 conversations a day" makes the point instantly and gives the chapter a memorable image.

*Prompt: Figure 1.1, the goal ladder.*

> [Shared style from README.] Title: "Figure 1.1. From an income goal to a daily number." A vertical funnel of seven stacked horizontal bars, each narrower than the one above it, widest at top. Top to bottom, each bar carries a label and an example value: "Income goal: $80,000", "Closings: 13 (÷ $6,500 per closing)", "Appointments: 39 (about 3 per closing)", "Conversations: about 780 (about 20 per appointment)", "Per week: about 16 (÷ 48 weeks)", "Per day: 3 to 4". Color the top two bars deep blue (#1E3A8A), the middle bars blue (#2563EB), and the bottom bar amber (#B45309) with the words "This is the only number you control today." To the right of the funnel add a thin bracket over the top three bars labelled "Lagging: results" and a bracket over the bottom three labelled "Leading: activity." Caption: "Work backward until you reach something you can do tomorrow morning."

A small second figure is optional: *leading versus lagging on a timeline* (activity today → closings 60–120 days later), showing the delay. Only add it if Figure 1.1 is not enough.

## Prose and accuracy

- **"Agents who treat your CRM as non-negotiable stay organized. Those who do not fall behind within a quarter and rarely recover the lost leads."** Two errors: "your CRM" should be "their CRM" (or rewrite in second person). "Rarely recover" is an unsupported claim. Suggested: "If you treat your CRM as non-negotiable, you stay organized. If you do not, you will fall behind within a quarter, and the leads you lose in that stretch are hard to win back."
- **"Real estate is a business, not a job."** Cliché. The paragraph after it is better. Consider cutting the slogan and opening with the sentence about Monday morning.
- **"Closings do not respond for 60 to 120 days"** (body) vs. **"lag by two to four months"** (Key Takeaway 1). Equivalent, but use one phrasing so the number sticks.
- **"Conversation" is defined only in the table caption** after the reader has met it three times. Move the definition into the paragraph before the table.
- **"Opportunities" (capitalised)** appears with no definition. First use should say "your Opportunities, the people you are actively working with, shown as a pipeline in your CRM."
- **Table 1.1 note "$6,500 average commission per closed side after splits"** is sound; add "per side" explanation (a "side" is one end of a transaction, buyer or seller).
- **Stress-test:** the interactive calculator adds a stress test (the review document notes the "Your number" column does not move with the buttons). Not a content issue here, but when the diagram is added, say in its caption whether the numbers are fixed.
