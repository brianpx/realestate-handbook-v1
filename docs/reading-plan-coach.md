# Reading plan coach: reminders, progress, and spaced review

Drafted 19 October 2026 against branch `next`. Nothing here has been built. File names, line numbers and word counts come from reading the repo; the learning-science choices are standard practice (retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving), not measurements from this handbook.

## What exists today

- The reading plan is a four-item list in `content/pages/pref.html` (Week 1: Ch 1, 4, 12; Week 2: Ch 2, 5, 6; Week 3: Ch 7, 8, 9; Week 4: Ch 3, 10, 11, 13). It is static text. Nothing tracks it, reminds anyone, or adapts to a start date.
- Each chapter ends with a "Test your knowledge" box: three multiple-choice questions plus one fill-in-the-blank (`KNOWLEDGE_CHECKS`, `legacy/js/widgets-practice.js:~866`). Scores are not stored, and questions carry no concept label.
- The drills (`choiceDrill`) already have an optional `tag` field, but it is a display label, not a concept id.
- Progress is a `read` map in `localStorage` (`handbookState`), marked when a sentinel scrolls into view. Scrolling past a chapter counts as reading it.
- Chapters are small: 308 to 1,298 words (about 10,700 total, so 2 to 7 minutes of reading each). **The time cost is the "Try it today" exercises, which the preface sizes at up to an hour.** A plan that schedules reading minutes alone will under-budget every week.
- Headings in `content/pages/*.html` have no `id` attributes, so nothing can deep-link to "the section about 8x8".
- Port status: the state layer (migration phase 2), widgets (phase 3) and accounts (phase 5) are not built yet. See `docs/nextjs-migration-plan.md`.

## Goal and design principles

The goal is that a new agent can still *do* the thing in week 8, not that they finished the book. That points to five principles:

1. **Retrieval beats rereading.** Quizzing is the main learning tool here, not a grading step at the end. Questions come after reading, then again on a schedule.
2. **Space it.** A concept missed today returns tomorrow, then in about 3 days, a week, two weeks, a month. Passing moves it out; missing pulls it back in.
3. **Mix it.** Weekly and final quizzes interleave chapters. Block-by-chapter quizzes feel easier and teach less.
4. **Send the learner back to the exact passage.** A wrong answer should land on the paragraph that answers it, not "reread Chapter 5".
5. **Reminders should lower the cost of starting.** One small, specific next action with a link that opens straight to it. No guilt, no streak-loss threats, and an easy way to slow down or stop.

Everything useful works with no account and no network. Reminders and cross-device sync need an identity; the learning loop does not.

## The learner experience

### 1. Start the plan

On first visit to the preface (and from a "My plan" link in the sidebar):

- Pick a **start date**, a **pace** (the 4-week default, a 2-week sprint, or an 8-week relaxed version), and **study days and time** (for example Mon/Wed/Fri, 7:30 a.m.).
- Optional **pre-test**: ten mixed questions drawn across all chapters. Skippable. It does two things: the learner sees what they already know, and the plan can mark chapters "skim" instead of "read".
- The plan expands the existing four weeks into dated **sessions**. Each session has a reading part (chapters), a *do* part (the Try-it exercise for those chapters, with a time estimate), and a *check* part (the quiz).
- Sessions are generated by one function from `{ startDate, pace, studyDays, timezone }`. The week-to-chapter mapping lives in `content/data/plan.ts`, so the preface list and the planner cannot drift apart.

### 2. Today

A page at `/plan` (and a small card at the top of the sidebar) shows only what is next:

> **Today: Chapter 4, Your CRM** · read 5 min · set up your pipeline 45 min · then 4 review questions

Below it: the review queue count, the week's sessions, and a "Reschedule" button. Falling behind **re-flows the remaining sessions** from today; it never shows a wall of red overdue items.

### 3. Follow progress

Three honest measures, shown separately, because "I read it" and "I know it" are different:

| Measure | Source | Shown as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read | scroll sentinel (existing) plus an explicit "Mark done" button, since scrolling past is not reading | chapter ticks, bar |
| Did | Try-it and checklist completion (existing widgets) | per-chapter dot |
| Know | concept mastery from quiz attempts | per-chapter strength meter, and a map of all concepts coloured by box |

Also show: on pace or N days behind (a fact, not a grade), sessions completed this week, and concept count by state (new, learning, reviewing, mastered). Skip streak counters: they punish a missed sick day and reward gaming.

### 4. Review what was hard

- **Chapter pages** get a small banner when concepts from that chapter are weak: "You missed 2 ideas here: Speed to lead, 8x8 cadence." Each links to the section and the section is marked with a side rule until the learner retests it.
- **Review queue** at `/review`: a short deck (5 to 10 questions, capped so it never becomes a chore) of whatever is due, mixed across chapters.
- **After a miss**, the feedback screen shows the explanation, then a "Read the passage" link (deep link to the anchor) and requeues the concept for later in the same session, then tomorrow.

## How the learning loop works

### Concept tags

A **concept** is one idea a question can test, small enough that "I know it" is a yes or no. Roughly 3 to 5 per chapter, about 50 total.

```ts
// content/data/concepts.ts
export type Concept = {
  id: string;          // 'ch5.touch-cadence'
  chapter: string;     // 'ch5'
  title: string;       // 'The 13-touch nurture cadence'
  summary: string;     // one sentence, shown in review and on the concept map
  anchor: string;      // 'nurture-cadence'  -> /ch5#nurture-cadence
};

// content/data/questions.ts
export type Question = {
  id: string;
  concept: string;     // Concept.id; one primary concept per question
  kind: 'mcq' | 'blank';
  // mcq: prompt, options, answer index; blank: before/after/answer/accept[]
  explain: string;     // existing explain text, kept
};
```

The existing `KNOWLEDGE_CHECKS` move into this shape, with a `concept` added to each item. Each concept needs **at least two questions** (ideally three, worded differently) or learners memorize the answer key instead of the idea. Today that is 3 questions per chapter for 3 to 5 concepts, so the item bank has to roughly double.

### Confidence before the answer

Before showing options, ask "How sure are you?" with three buttons: *Guessing / Think so / Sure*. This is cheap and valuable:

- A confident wrong answer is the most useful signal in the system (the learner will correct it more strongly when shown it, and it predicts real-world errors). Treat it as a hard miss: back to box 1 and flag the concept.
- A correct guess does **not** advance the concept. Lucky answers should not graduate material.
- Confidence replaces the existing end-of-chapter "confidence" idea with one that feeds the scheduler.

### Mastery and scheduling (a small Leitner scheme)

Each concept the learner has seen has `box` (0 to 5), `due` (a date), and a short attempt history. Boxes map to intervals; this needs no ML and is easy to unit-test.

| Box | Meaning | Next review after a correct answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | seen, never answered | same session |
| 1 | missed recently | 1 day |
| 2 | | 3 days |
| 3 | | 7 days |
| 4 | | 14 days |
| 5 | mastered | 30 days, then drops out of the daily queue |

```ts
function grade(c: ConceptState, correct: boolean, confidence: 'guess' | 'think' | 'sure', now: Date) {
  if (!correct) return { box: confidence === 'sure' ? 1 : Math.max(1, c.box - 2), due: addDays(now, 1), flagged: true };
  if (confidence === 'guess') return { box: c.box, due: addDays(now, 1), flagged: c.flagged };   // no credit for a lucky guess
  return { box: Math.min(5, c.box + 1), due: addDays(now, INTERVALS[c.box + 1]), flagged: false };
}
```

Rules that keep it from getting annoying:

- **Cap a daily queue at 10 questions.** Overflow waits. Drop oldest-due first when the learner is far behind.
- **Serve a different question** for the same concept than last time when one exists.
- **Interleave**: sort the day's deck so no more than two questions from one chapter run in a row.
- **Same-session re-test** of a missed concept after other questions (at least 3 later), not immediately, so it tests memory rather than echo.

### Three kinds of assessment

| Assessment | When | Length | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chapter check (exists) | end of each chapter | 3 mcq + 1 blank | first retrieval, creates concept states |
| Weekly mixed quiz | last session of each week | 10 questions, interleaved, weighted toward weak or due concepts | spaced re-test plus a cross-chapter read on the week |
| Final assessment | after the last session | about 25 questions, every concept at least once, scenario-style mcq where possible | readiness picture and the "go back to" list |

The final result is not pass or fail. It is a ranked **"revisit these"** list of concepts with passage links, and it seeds the long-tail review queue. A learner with five weak concepts keeps getting nudged on exactly those five for a month.

Scenario questions ("A buyer texts at 9 p.m. Day 2 of your nurture plan. What do you send?") are worth more than recall questions for the final. Writing them is a content job, listed below.

## Reminders

### What gets sent

| Kind | Trigger | Channel default | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Session | morning of a scheduled study day, at the learner's chosen time | email; SMS if they opted in | "Today: Chapter 4 (about 50 min including the CRM setup). Open it: <link>" |
| Review due | 5 or more questions due and none answered in 24 hours | SMS if opted in, else email | "5 quick review questions are ready (3 min): <link>" |
| Catch-up | 3 missed study days | email only | "Want to move your plan? Pick a new pace: <link>" (re-plans; does not scold) |
| Weekly summary | end of week | email only | what you finished, what to revisit, next week's chapters |
| Milestone | week quiz done, final done | email | what changed since the pre-test |

Hard rules: at most **one message per day per channel**, quiet hours in the learner's timezone (no sends before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. local), a one-click pause ("pause for 2 weeks"), and a way to change frequency without unsubscribing. If the learner has opened the app that day, skip the nudge.

### Making the link do the work

Every message deep-links to the exact next thing (`/plan/today`, `/review`), authenticated by a short-lived signed token so the learner is not stopped at a login page on a phone. The token is single-purpose (it can open the review page and record answers, nothing else) and expires.

### SendGrid (email) and Twilio (SMS)

Both are called from server code only; keys are never `NEXT_PUBLIC_*`.

- **SendGrid:** authenticate the sending domain (SPF, DKIM, and a DMARC record) before the first send, or reminders land in spam and the whole feature quietly fails. Use a dedicated sender subdomain. Every email carries an unsubscribe link and a physical mailing address (CAN-SPAM). Subscribe to the **Event Webhook** (bounce, spam report, unsubscribe) and write those back to the user's channel status.
- **Twilio:** use a **Messaging Service**, not a loose number. US application-to-person texting requires **A2P 10DLC brand and campaign registration**, which can take days or weeks to approve, so start it first. Point Twilio's inbound webhook at us so `STOP`, `START` and `HELP` update our consent record (Twilio blocks sending to a number that sent STOP, but our table must agree so the UI is truthful).
- **Both webhooks verify the provider's signature** before touching the database. Both are idempotent.
- Wrap each provider in a small interface (`sendEmail(msg)`, `sendSms(msg)`) so the scheduler never imports a vendor SDK. Both providers have plain REST APIs, so `fetch` may be enough and avoid a dependency. That is your call; see open questions.

### Consent (and the irony)

The handbook's own review flagged that the book prescribes automated texts without a consent step. The coach must not repeat that.

- Email and SMS are **separate, unchecked opt-ins**. Phone number is asked only when SMS is chosen.
- SMS uses **double opt-in**: learner enters a number, receives "Reply YES to get study reminders from <name>. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to quit." and nothing else is sent until YES.
- Store the exact consent text shown, a timestamp, and the IP or user agent, per channel (the `consent` table below).
- Honor quiet hours, honor STOP immediately and in every channel the learner associates with it, and keep the opt-out record even after account deletion (so we never re-send).
- Have counsel review the SMS wording and registration before launch. This doc is a design, not legal advice.

## Architecture

It rides on the migration plan, not beside it.

### State and storage

Extend the typed store from migration phase 2 with three slices (same zod-validated, try/catch-guarded `localStorage` pattern):

```ts
plan:     { startDate, pace, studyDays: number[], time: string, tz: string, sessions: Session[] }
concepts: Record<ConceptId, { box: number; due: string; flagged: boolean; seen: number; history: Attempt[] }>
attempts: Attempt[]   // { questionId, correct, confidence, at }, capped (for example last 500)
```

Anonymous learners get the whole learning loop on-device. Signing in syncs these slices through the phase 5 `progress` table (per-key JSON, newest-wins, never silently overwrite). The scheduler only ever reads from the server copy, so **reminders require an account and a verified email.**

### Server tables (Neon)

```sql
reminder_prefs (
  user_id      uuid primary key references users,
  tz           text not null,
  email_on     boolean not null default false,
  sms_on       boolean not null default false,
  phone_e164   text,                          -- only when sms_on
  send_time    time not null default '07:30',
  study_days   smallint[] not null,
  paused_until date
);

consent (
  id          bigserial primary key,
  user_id     uuid not null references users,
  channel     text not null check (channel in ('email','sms')),
  action      text not null check (action in ('opt_in','confirm','opt_out')),
  text_shown  text not null,
  source      text not null,                   -- 'web', 'sms_reply', 'sendgrid_unsub'
  ip          inet,
  at          timestamptz not null default now()
);

reminder_sends (
  user_id        uuid not null,
  kind           text not null,
  channel        text not null,
  scheduled_for  timestamptz not null,
  status         text not null,                -- queued, sent, failed, skipped
  provider_id    text,
  primary key (user_id, kind, channel, scheduled_for)   -- the idempotency guard
);
```

### Scheduling

A cron route (for example `GET /api/cron/reminders`, every 15 minutes) selects users whose local send time falls in the window, who are not paused, are consented, and have not already been sent that kind today; applies the skip rules (opened the app today, daily cap, quiet hours); and inserts into `reminder_sends` with the primary key as the guard before calling the provider. A duplicate cron run then hits a unique violation instead of double-sending. Secure the route with a cron secret.

Check the current Vercel plan limits on cron frequency before relying on 15 minutes; if only daily is available, move to Vercel Queues or an external scheduler. For a few hundred learners, a single sweep is enough. If volume grows, fan out through a queue so one slow provider call cannot stall the sweep.

### Routes

All validate input with zod, take the user id from the session (never the request), and re-check authorization server-side.

- `POST /api/coach/prefs` (create or update preferences, writes `consent` rows)
- `POST /api/coach/sms/verify` (double opt-in start; confirmation happens via the Twilio webhook)
- `POST /api/coach/progress` (sync, from phase 5)
- `GET /api/cron/reminders`
- `POST /api/webhooks/twilio`, `POST /api/webhooks/sendgrid` (signature-verified)
- `GET /r/[token]` (signed deep link, redirects to the session or review page)

### Components

Server by default; `"use client"` at the leaves that need state: `PlanSetup`, `TodayCard`, `ReviewDeck`, `ConfidencePrompt`, `ConceptMap`, `ChapterWeakBanner`. The scheduling math (`buildPlan`, `grade`, `dueQueue`, `interleave`) is pure TypeScript in `lib/coach/` with Vitest coverage, including time-zone and month-end cases. Tests of the scheduler are where the bugs will live, as with the post-close scheduler in the migration plan.

## Content work that has to happen

1. **Fix the contradictions before tagging them** (`docs/handbook-webpage-review.md`): the 8x8 definition, 8 to 12 versus 13 touches, and the Chapter 2 checkpoint mismatch. A concept tag on a contradictory idea makes a quiz that teaches the wrong answer.
2. **Add anchors**: a stable `id` on every `h2` and `h3` that a concept points to. Done by a one-time script plus a unit test that every `Concept.anchor` exists in its page, so renaming a heading cannot silently break a review link.
3. **Write the item bank**: aim for 3 questions per concept (about 150 total from today's 52), at least a quarter scenario-style, and 25 for the final. This is the biggest cost in the project and should be reviewed by someone who has worked as an agent.
4. **Time estimates**: a per-chapter "minutes" figure for reading and for the Try-it exercise, written by hand (the exercise times vary too much to compute).

## Phases

Ordered by learning value per unit of effort. The first three need no vendor, no account and no database.

| Phase | Deliverable | Depends on | Rough size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Concept registry, anchors, tagged and expanded question bank, `grade`/`dueQueue` with tests | migration phase 2 (store) | 3 to 5 days, mostly content |
| B | Plan setup, `/plan` Today view, three progress measures, in-app only | A, migration phase 3 widgets | 2 to 3 days |
| C | `/review` deck, confidence prompt, chapter weak banners, weekly and final assessments, the "revisit these" list | A, B | 3 to 4 days |
| D | Accounts and progress sync | migration phase 5 | counted in that phase |
| E | Email reminders: SendGrid, consent, cron, webhook, unsubscribe | D | 2 to 3 days, plus domain authentication lead time |
| F | SMS reminders: Twilio, double opt-in, STOP/HELP sync | E | 2 to 3 days, plus 10DLC registration lead time |

Why this order: the habit loop (plan, retrieve, space, revisit) is what teaches. Reminders amplify a loop that already works, and a reminder that links to a thin experience just trains people to ignore the next one. Start the SendGrid domain setup and the Twilio 10DLC registration during phase B anyway, because their approval times, not the code, will gate E and F.

Estimates are rough. Re-estimate after phase A, since the item bank is the uncertain part.

## How we will know it works

Track, per learner, only what answers a question:

- **Plan completion**: share of scheduled sessions finished within 2 days of their date, by whether reminders are on. This is the direct test of the reminder feature.
- **Reminder to action**: percentage of sends followed by app activity within 24 hours, by kind and channel. Cut the kinds that nobody acts on.
- **Learning**: concept correctness on first attempt versus the final assessment, and 30-day retention on mastered concepts. Pre-test versus final is the headline number.
- **Harm**: opt-out and unsubscribe rate per kind. A rising rate means cadence is too high.

Do this with aggregate counts and no new third-party analytics unless you ask for one. The quiz and confidence data are personal; offer "delete my data" and mention it in the footer.

## Risks

- **Reminder fatigue.** Mitigated by the daily cap, skip-if-active, pause, and fewer kinds by default. Start with Session and Weekly only; add Review-due after seeing the numbers.
- **Item-bank quality.** A poor question teaches a wrong thing quickly. Needs expert review and a "report this question" link feeding a short list.
- **Over-gamifying.** No streaks, leaderboards or badges in v1. The motivator is visible competence on a real job.
- **SMS compliance and deliverability.** Unregistered or badly worded campaigns get filtered or blocked. Treat registration and consent copy as launch blockers for phase F, not follow-ups.
- **Identity gap.** Reminders cannot work for anonymous learners. The ask for an email must say why (to remind you), not look like the old gate.
- **Time zones.** Store `tz` explicitly; do not infer from the server. Test the DST weeks.

## Open questions

1. **Account model for reminders.** Wait for the full phase 5 accounts (Neon Auth), or ship a lighter email-magic-link sign-in earlier that phase 5 later absorbs? The second gets reminders to learners sooner but means two identity paths briefly.
2. **Dependencies.** Official `@sendgrid/mail` and `twilio` SDKs, or plain `fetch` against their REST APIs plus manual webhook-signature checks? (Per your rule I have not added anything.)
3. **Scheduler.** Is Vercel Cron on your plan frequent enough, or should this use Vercel Queues or an external scheduler?
4. **Who authors the item bank**, and who reviews it for accuracy?
5. **SMS in v1 or later?** Registration lead time and consent overhead suggest email first, SMS once there is evidence that email nudges work.
6. **Sender identity.** What name, domain and mailing address appear on messages and on the "Reply YES" text?
7. **Final assessment stakes.** Private readiness report only (recommended), or a certificate or completion record someone else sees? The second changes how careful the question quality and anti-cheating need to be.
